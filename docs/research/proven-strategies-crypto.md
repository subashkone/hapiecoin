# Build Trend Playbooks, Shelve the Lottery Tickets

Status: deep research for the user, 8 November 2026. Read-only: nothing in the app changed. Six researcher note files are in [proven-strategies-crypto-notes/](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/) and the verification scripts with their raw outputs in [scripts/proven-strategies/](scripts/proven-strategies/) (data caches are not in git). Companions: [strategy-playbooks.md](strategy-playbooks.md), [backtest-daily-structures.md](backtest-daily-structures.md).

Of the eleven strategy families examined, exactly one is both proven in traditional markets and confirmed on Delta Exchange's own history: **long/flat trend following on BTC with a 40-150 day lookback**, which on 2020-2026 mark closes after fees, slippage and the exchange's actual funding delivered a **Sharpe of 1.12 (SMA 50) against 0.73 for buy-and-hold and cut the maximum drawdown from 77 % to 59 %**, with the parameter sweep showing two broad plateaus (40-70 and 100-150 days) rather than one lucky peak ([Verification, Part A](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). Everything else is weaker than its reputation. "Hero or zero" expiry-day buying is negative in every dataset: **88-93 % of Indian F&O retail lose money in every SEBI study since FY22**, US retail lost **$70-125 million on 0DTE SPX options**, and on **1,426 Delta daily-option positions the buyer lost 46 % of premium once a single 21 September 2026 rally is removed** ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html); [CXO on Beckmeyer et al.](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance); [Verification, Part B](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). Premium selling rests on a real but shrinking premium: BTC implied-minus-realised volatility fell from **13.2 vol points (2021-23) to 3.4 (2024-26)**, no public crypto strangle or condor backtest with fees exists, and the mirror seller in our test won 88-100 % of days while its worst day ran **37 to 560 times the average credit** ([Vine](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper1.html); [Verification, Part B](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). Carry, mean reversion, funding signals, seasonality, equity-style factors, on-chain indicators and macro-event trades either have edges smaller than Delta India's **11.8 bp taker round trip** on perpetuals or cannot be expressed with three underlyings and no spot leg. The verdict for HapieCoin: build a trend-following playbook (long/flat, medium lookback, defined-risk option expression as an option not a default), build premium harvest only on weekly or monthly expiries and only after an in-house backtest on `chain_eod` clears Delta's fee rule, keep daily-expiry selling to the final-hours window, and do not ship hero-or-zero, naked daily strangles, mean reversion or funding-contrarian playbooks.

## "Proven" means a plateau, a second team and a post-2020 sample, not a high Sharpe

The overfitting literature sets the bar this report applies. Published equity return predictors earned **26 % less out of sample and 58 % less after publication** across 97 anomalies, and a 2022 follow-up found gross anomaly returns decay about **50 % post-publication, 72 % on post-2005 data and 93 % after total trading costs** ([CXO on McLean-Pontiff](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/effects-of-market-adaptation); [Quantitative Finance 2022](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2022.2098810)). Harvey, Liu and Zhu counted 316 published factors and concluded a new one needs a **t-statistic above 3.0, not 2.0** ([Harvey-Liu-Zhu](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:20592)). Bailey and López de Prado showed that testing **more than about 45 configurations on five years of data almost guarantees an in-sample Sharpe of 1 with an expected out-of-sample Sharpe of zero**, and proposed the deflated Sharpe ratio to correct for both trial count and fat tails ([Bailey et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [Deflated Sharpe](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). Crypto already shows the same decay: in the 2013-2025 re-estimates by Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu the size premium weakened from t = −2.66 to −1.69 after 2020, 12- and 24-week momentum never worked (t = 0.47 and −1.50), the carry Sharpe fell from **6.45 (2020-25) to 4.06 from 2024 and negative in 2025**, and the cross-exchange arbitrage spread of 0.685 % per week on all pairs shrinks to 0.038 % on investable ones ([Borri et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)).

So "proven" in this report means seven things at once: an economic mechanism (risk compensation or a structural constraint, not a pattern), replication by at least a second research team, survival in a sample the original authors never saw (for crypto, the post-2020 regime), costs at the venue's real fee schedule, a parameter plateau rather than a single peak, a disclosed trial count or deflated Sharpe, and no dependence on one day or one month for the sign of the result. Crypto's short history binds hard: BTC has roughly twelve years of liquid daily data and six post-2020, so **monthly rules such as Faber's 10-month SMA or 12-month absolute momentum cannot be statistically proven on BTC at all** (about 70 post-2020 observations), while daily and weekly rules can be, provided few trials are run ([Borri et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)). The confidence labels used below follow from this. **High** means peer-reviewed in traditional markets, replicated in crypto by two or more teams with costs, and confirmed on our own Delta data. **Medium** means peer-reviewed or multi-firm evidence in one market but single-source, gross-of-cost or unverified in the other. **Low** means practitioner, blog or single-paper evidence, or evidence that our own test contradicts.

A note on what our own verification can carry. The empirical verification was computed on 8 November 2026 from Delta's public candle endpoints (global exchange 2019-2023 spliced to Delta India from its 18 December 2023 launch), the exchange's own `FUNDING:` symbol rather than an assumed 0.01 % per 8 hours, product-list fees, and the existing 5-minute mark cache of 90 BTC daily expiries from 8 August to 5 November 2026 ([Verification, data section](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). Its limits are stated once here and apply everywhere below: mark prices are model prices with no bid under them, so every option fill flatters the buyer and the seller; margin, liquidation and the maker rebate are not modelled; the option sample is 90 days in one regime with one large up-move and no large down-move; the BTC trend sample is one cycle; and no bootstrap or significance test was run, so the dispersion evidence is the yearly columns and the parameter sweep. A Sharpe difference of 0.2-0.3 between neighbouring lookbacks is within noise on 60-140 trades.

## Trend following is the only family both proven elsewhere and confirmed here, and only long/flat at 40-150 days

Time-series momentum is the best-documented return pattern in finance by breadth rather than by magnitude. Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen found 12-month trend profits "positive, not just on average across these assets, but for every asset contract we examine (58 in total)" over 1985-2009, and Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen extended the record to 1880-2013 across 67 markets with **14.9 % gross, 11.2 % net of 2/20 fees, 9.7 % volatility and a net Sharpe of 0.77, positive in every decade**, while modelling a conservative future Sharpe of only 0.4 and documenting net drawdowns up to 26 % with recoveries of up to 75 months ([MOP 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250); [AQR century paper](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/03/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing.pdf)). The live record is lumpier: the SG Trend Index Sharpe fell to about **0.05 over 2010-2018**, the SG CTA Index returned **+20.1 % in 2022** (its best year) and the SG Trend Index **−4.16 % in 2023** ([CME Group](https://cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/the-market-environment-for-trend-following.html); [The Full FX](https://thefullfx.com/?p=3378); [Top Traders Unplugged](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/?p=14211)). Trend is therefore proven as a long-run premium with multi-year flat spells, and most of its historical Sharpe comes from diversification across 50-70 markets that a single-coin implementation forfeits.

The crypto literature agrees on the shape of the result and disagrees on its size. Detzel and co-authors report that long-BTC-when-above-SMA rules (5-100 days) over 2010-2017 raised the Sharpe from 2.0 to 2.2-2.6 and cut the maximum drawdown from 89.5 % to 66-70 %, but gross of costs and in a period when BTC compounded at 224 % a year ([CXO on Detzel et al.](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/currency-trading/timing-bitcoin-with-smas/)). The Monash study is the only academic source with a net figure: **BTC 20-day SMA gross Sharpe 1.68, net 1.25 at 0.5 % per trade, 2016-2023** ([CXO on Le and Ruthbah](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/applying-simple-trend-following-rules-to-cryptocurrencies)). Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon report a Donchian ensemble with volatility sizing on the top-20 liquid coins at a net Sharpe above 1.5 and 10.8 % annual alpha over bitcoin since 2015 ([Barbon](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends)), and Man AHL finds moving-average and breakout models on 10-15 coins Sharpe-positive after transaction, shorting and liquidity costs ([Man AHL](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend)). The warnings matter as much: Rozario and co-authors found "no predictable and attractive Sharpe ratios" when hourly MA windows optimised on one year were applied to the next ([Rozario et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12155)); Hudson and Urquhart found in-sample predictability for 14,919 rules but "no predictability for Bitcoin" out of sample ([Hudson and Urquhart](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/85715)); and 2025 was a textbook whipsaw year, with realised volatility at 43 %, 30-day returns flipping sign repeatedly, and the 10 October cascade liquidating about $19 billion in 24 hours, before crypto trend funds rebounded in early 2026 as BTC fell more than 10 % ([XBTO](https://www.xbto.com/resources/2025-the-year-market-regime-mattered-more-than-market-direction); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-27/crypto-trend-following-trade-finds-relief-after-sharp-selloff)).

Our own test is the first of these to use Delta's data, Delta's fee schedule and the exchange's actual funding. On BTC mark closes from 1 January 2020 to 7 November 2026, with 0.07 % per side per unit of position change and the real 8-hour funding paid when long, **SMA 50 long/flat returned +1,240 % (CAGR 46.1 %) at a Sharpe of 1.12 and a maximum drawdown of 59 %, against buy-and-hold's +478 % (29.2 %), 0.73 and 77 %**; TSMOM 12-1 long/flat reached 0.98 with the smallest drawdown (53 %); SMA 200 long/flat and Donchian 20 long/flat earned less than buy-and-hold in total (+296 % and +533 %) but with drawdowns of 61-66 % ([Verification, Part A](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). The MA sweep is the strongest anti-overfitting evidence in the whole exercise: **every long/flat length from 30 to 170 days beats buy-and-hold's after-funding Sharpe, lengths 180-300 fall below it, and every length from 10 to 300 has a smaller drawdown than 77 %**, with the best single lengths at 40 (Sharpe 1.22, CAGR +51.6 %, MaxDD −50 %) and 110 (1.08, +44.5 %, −30 %) and clear losers at 10-20 days (Sharpe 0.54-0.56, 296 trades). That is two plateaus, not a spike, and it contradicts the two lengths the literature quotes most often for crypto: the 200-day (0.68 here, and an 82.4 % drawdown lasting 1,662 days in a 50/200 crossover study) and anything under 30 days ([Verification, Part A](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md); [50/200 study](https://crypto-threads-five.vercel.app/post/bitcoin-death-cross-a-data-driven-performance-study)). In the India-era window alone (2024-2026), every long/flat rule still beat buy-and-hold on Sharpe and drawdown but only SMA 50 did so by a wide margin (0.76 vs 0.43, MaxDD −29 % vs −56 %).

Three conditions fall directly out of the data. First, **long/flat, not long/short**: on BTC no length's long/short version beat its long/flat Sharpe, SMA 20 and SMA 200 long/short lost money outright (−68 % and −21 % total), and this is despite the short leg receiving funding, which flatters it. Second, **the asset matters more than the rule**: on ETH (August 2022-2026) only SMA 40-60 (Sharpe 0.61-0.73 vs 0.29 buy-and-hold) and a weaker 130-200 bump cleared buy-and-hold, while **TSMOM 12-1 lost 65 % long/flat and 95 % long/short**, the opposite of its BTC result, because it stayed long through ETH's 2025-2026 declines and short through the recoveries. ETH's window also starts after its main bull market, so its baseline is unusually poor and its results are not comparable to BTC's. Third, **funding is the cost that matters**: fees and slippage removed 1-2 CAGR points from every rule at 59-137 trades over 6.85 years (4 points for SMA 20's 296 trades), while actual funding removed **9-12 points from every long-biased rule and from buy-and-hold**, and the 2021 funding alone cost a permanent long about 21 % that year ([Verification, Part A](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). The literature's funding numbers are consistent: a record 66 % annualised in January 2024 and 16 % on average in Q1 2024 ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2024/01/02/bitcoin-bullish-bets-costlier-than-ever-as-funding-rates-hit-record-66/)). A long-only BTC trend rule on a perpetual pays its drag precisely when it is long, in bull markets, which is the same order of magnitude as the excess return trend adds.

Two conditions come from the literature and were not tested here. Volatility targeting is the single largest reported improvement: in the one direct crypto comparison, vol-scaled TSMOM at a 10 % target went from Sharpe 0.54-0.65 and drawdowns of 35-38 % to **Sharpe 1.83 and −16.1 %** over 2022-2024 on Binance perpetuals, in an unreviewed preprint with arithmetic inconsistencies ([Talyxion](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)), and a 20 % vol cap on a BTC-gold composite cut the drawdown from 49 % to 12 % at almost unchanged Sharpe (1.47 to 1.37) ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/dual-momentum-allocation-between-physical-gold-and-bitcoin-digital-gold/?a=6080)). Harvey and co-authors show the Sharpe gain holds for "risk assets" with a leverage effect, which BTC has, and that left tails shrink everywhere ([Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). The honest statement is that vol targeting is proven to cut drawdowns and plausibly raises Sharpe on BTC, and that this remains to be run on our data. The second untested condition is the options expression. Trend following is mechanically a long-straddle replication (a 12-month S&P trend rule and a delta-replicated straddle had 95.8 % monthly correlation), but every source concludes the futures version is cheaper because trend pays realised-variance whipsaw cost while options pay the implied-variance premium, and most of trend's historical return comes from the trading-impact term an option buyer never captures ([Newfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/02/trend-convexity-premium/); [Quantpedia on Dao et al.](https://quantpedia.com/tail-protection-of-trend-following-strategies/)). For HapieCoin that means a call-spread or put-spread trend playbook should expect lower average return than the perp version, with better behaviour in gap events like 10 October 2025 that a daily-rebalanced perp rule cannot hedge, and that it is most defensible when implied volatility is low relative to realised. No backtest of trend expressed through BTC or ETH options exists anywhere; that is the obvious next run.

## Expiry-day options: buying lost 46 % outside one day, selling won 88-100 % of days and still lost

The prior from theory and from regulators could hardly be stronger. Coval and Shumway's zero-beta ATM S&P straddles lose about **3 % per week**; Boyer and Vorkink's skewness-sorted option portfolios differ by **10-50 % per week**; Ni's OTM single-stock calls fall to about **−37 % per month** at the highest moneyness ([Coval-Shumway](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:56:y:2001:i:3:p:983-1009); [Boyer-Vorkink](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v69y2014i4p1485-1527.html)). Hero-or-zero is the extreme point of that curve. SEBI's series puts the loss share of individual F&O traders at **89 % (FY22), 93 % (FY22-24), 91 % (FY25) and 87.7 % (FY26)**, with options driving about 92 % of losses, **82.1 % losing even before transaction costs**, and the expiry-day share of individual index-option turnover at 70 % in FY25 and 59 % in FY26 ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)). SEBI's October 2024 package then cut weekly expiries to one index per exchange, raised contract size to Rs 15-20 lakh and added a 2 % extreme-loss margin on short options on expiry day ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/sebis-new-rules-for-index-derivatives-heres-whats-changing)). The only retail-level 0DTE P&L study, on Cboe SPX data from January 2021 to February 2023, found aggregate retail losses of about $70 million (later vintages cite $125 million) with the daily loss roughly doubling to about $358,000 once daily expirations began in May 2022 ([CXO on Beckmeyer et al.](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance)). The buyer is also fighting a decay rate tastylive measured at **26 cents per dollar of premium between 8:30 and 10:40 CST on a 0DTE SPX straddle, against 0.3 cents at 45 DTE** ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/theta-0dte-options)).

Nothing in the crypto literature tests daily options directly; our verification fills that hole for one venue and one quarter. Across 90 BTC daily expiries from 8 August to 5 November 2026, buying 0.1 BTC of 1, 2 and 3 % OTM calls and puts at 02:00, 06:00 and 09:00 UTC and holding to settlement produced **1,426 positions with a 2.8 % hit rate and a total of −$124 on $2,649 of premium**; calls made +$821 and puts lost $946 in every cell at every hour ([Verification, Part B](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). The headline hides the structure. The single best day, 21 September 2026 (spot +4.49 % from 02:00 to settlement), produced +$1,065 across 16 positions, with the 82000C bought at $14.98 settling for +$269 (18x) and the 83200C returning 101.8x; **without that day the buyer lost $1,189, or 46 % of premium, on 1,410 positions**. The 09:00 UTC (14:30 IST) entries are economically dead for both sides, with premiums of 4 cents to $1 per 0.1 BTC and hit rates of 0-2 %. The realised-move table explains why: from 02:00 UTC the mean absolute move to settlement was 0.64 %, only 3 % of days moved more than 2 %, and the biggest down move in 90 days was −1.91 %, never enough to put a 2 % put in the money. Whether hero-or-zero has "positive expectancy" therefore depends entirely on how often 4-5 % expiry-day rallies occur, and 90 days contain exactly one. Delta's own listing rule also limits the lottery supply: D1 strikes are added every five minutes to cover a 0.2-0.8 delta band, so 3 % OTM strikes existed on only 57-71 of 90 expiries ([Delta options guide](https://guides.delta.exchange/delta-exchange-india-user-guide/derivatives-guide/options-guide.md); [Verification, Part B](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)).

The seller's side is the more important finding for a product that already has 48 structures. In the US, 0DTE selling has a thin, real edge: Option Alpha's 25,000+ automated trades show iron butterflies winning 72 % and iron condors 63 %, but condors opened within two hours of the open averaged −0.36 % against +37 % for afternoon entries; FlashAlpha's 193 SPY Wednesday sessions (July 2022-April 2026) show the 10:00 ET straddle's implied band containing the close **71.0 % of the time against 68.3 % fair, a median realised/implied ratio of 0.59 against 0.67 fair (about 13 % overpricing)**, and one day, 9 April 2025, when SPY closed +8.72 % against a 3.51 % implied move; a naive 0DTE SPX condor over January-August 2022 lost at a −60 % annualised rate; and none of these include commissions ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/0dte); [FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/are-0dte-straddles-overpriced-193-spy-sessions-data-study); [Options Trading IQ](https://optionstradingiq.com/?p=28544)). Cboe reports that only 4 % of SPX 0DTE trades are naked short options and over 95 % are limited-risk ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)). Our mirror seller tells the same story in crypto numbers: **win rates of 88-100 %, a total of +$935 over 1,410 positions excluding 21 September, and on that day a loss of $273 against a $14.98 credit on one option**; sellers of 1 % and 2 % calls at 02:00 and 06:00 UTC lost money in total (−$100 to −$295 per cell) despite winning 88-98 % of days, and the worst day ran **37x the average credit for 1 % calls at 02:00, 94x for 2 % calls, 160x for 3 % calls and 560x for 3 % calls at 06:00** ([Verification, Part B](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). Put sellers made money in every cell for the mirror reason that there was no large down day. The companion 90-day structure backtest reached the same conclusion from the other direction: **every one of eight daily structures (iron butterfly, condor, naked strangle, ladder, 1:2:1 butterfly, two ratios, debit condor) lost on average at every exit rule, the only reliably non-losing window was 14:00-16:00 IST, the worst was 17:00-19:00 IST, and the 21:30 IST naked strangle's positive mean came from one +$485 month followed by a −$323 month with losing days ten times the average win** (`docs/research/backtest-daily-structures.md`).

The conditions under which daily-expiry selling can appear in a playbook are therefore narrow and specific: defined risk only (the seller's worst day is otherwise unbounded and margin was never modelled), entry in the final one to three and a half hours before the 17:30 IST settlement, hold to expiry because Delta charges no settlement fee on an option that expires worthless, never sell the new expiry at 17:30-19:30 IST as the US session opens, and accept that the credit is a few dollars at 100 lots with the 3.5 % fee cap plus GST taking about 4 % of it. The "pin butterfly" and "expiry-week credit spread" ideas in `docs/research/strategy-playbooks.md` should be read against this: the 1:2:1 butterfly centred at the money won on 29-34 % of days and lost on average, the max-pain "60 %" statistic has no identifiable primary source, and the only academic pinning result is for single stocks, not indices ([Verification, Part B](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). A weekly credit spread was outside both tests and remains open.

## Premium selling at 30-45 days: the premium is real, has compressed to three vol points, and no crypto strangle backtest exists

The equity put-write record is the model for every "premium harvest" playbook. Bondarenko's Cboe-commissioned study of the PUT index over July 1986-December 2015 found **10.1 % compound return against 9.8 % for the S&P 500 at 10.1 % versus 15.3 % volatility, a gross Sharpe of 0.67 versus 0.47, skewness of −2.09 versus −0.79, and a maximum drawdown of 32.7 % versus 50.9 %** ([CXO on Bondarenko](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes)). The premium survived 2018, 2020 and 2022, but a 2026 analysis finds PUT lagged buy-and-hold by about 4.5 % a year over 2020-2026, and every blow-up (short-vol ETPs losing over 90 % on 5 February 2018; Allianz Structured Alpha liquidating about $2.3 billion in March 2020) involved leverage or uncollateralised exposure rather than the fully collateralised index construction ([Vine](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper1.html); [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2021/volmageddon-failure-short-volatility-products)). The edge, in other words, is lower volatility and drawdown rather than higher return, and it is index-level, monthly, ATM and gross of costs.

Crypto has the premium, and more of it, at least historically. Almeida and co-authors measure a bitcoin variance risk premium of **0.14 in annualised variance units (implied 0.72 against realised 0.58) on Deribit from July 2017 to December 2022, against about 0.02 for the S&P 500**, with the sign flipping briefly in March-April 2020 and **38.66 % of the premium sitting in the +20 % to +60 % return region (52.3 % in low-volatility regimes)**, meaning calls carry real premium in crypto in a way they do not in equities ([Almeida et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15195v2)). The decisive finding for product design is the compression since. A self-published but fully specified DVOL study over March 2021-June 2026 finds BTC implied minus forward 30-day realised averaged **+8.9 vol points and was positive on 74 % of days (equities: 86 %), but fell from 13.2 points in the early window to 3.4 in the late one (ETH from 11.4 to 0.2), and the short-variance harvest Sharpe fell from 2.82 (2021-23) to 0.27 (2024-26)**, with the result turning negative under range-based realised-volatility estimators ([Vine](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper1.html)). Fewer positive days than equities means more losing windows for a crypto seller, and the 2024-2026 premium barely covers the 2-vol-point round-trip cost the author assumes.

The strategy-level evidence is thinner than the premium-level evidence. The only quantified crypto backtest with parameters is CryptoVol's BTC covered call over 7 June 2024-8 May 2026: **monthly 50-delta +27.6 % against HODL +12.2 % with a maximum drawdown of 26.1 % against 46.6 %, while weekly 10- and 25-delta versions underperformed HODL**, at mid fills with no fees and 22 monthly rolls ([CryptoVol](https://cryptovol.vercel.app/blog/btc-covered-call-backtest)). Covered-call ETFs lagged spot badly in the 2024-2025 rally. **No public backtest of a crypto short strangle or iron condor with stated DTE, delta and fees exists**, which means any "proven" claim for a HapieCoin premium-harvest playbook must come from the project's own `chain_eod` data. The equity parameter evidence that would seed that test is practitioner-grade and mostly gross: 45 DTE with 16-20 delta short strikes, higher deltas only when IV rank is high, IV rank above 50 improving win rate and credit ([tastylive mega strangle study](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/mega-strangle-study-05-12-2014)); and the "manage at 50 % or 21 DTE" rule, which raises win rate and P&L per day in tastylive's own studies but lost to a passive 30 DTE entry / 5 DTE exit on CAGR and Sharpe (4.44 % and 0.52 versus 5.46 % and 0.67, SPX 16-delta, 2001-2020, no costs) in an independent test ([SteadyOptions](https://steadyoptions.com/articles/does-%E2%80%9Cmanaging-winners%E2%80%9D-add-value-to-short-strangles-r618/)). Three crypto-specific adjustments follow. Skew is regime-dependent and often call-rich (BTC spot-IV correlation was −0.42 in 2019-20, then +0.74 for five months, then 0.08 in 2022), so the equity habit of selling the put side does not transfer and the rich wing should be read off the 25-delta risk reversal ([Harbourfront on Alexander and Imeraj](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/how-bitcoin-options-compare-to-equity)). IV rank is confounded by the secular compression of DVOL from 2021 to 2026. And the exchange itself is the tail: Deribit's BTC insurance fund roughly halved in three days in March 2020 and the exchange injected 500 BTC to avoid socialised losses ([Deribit](https://insights.deribit.com/exchange-updates/march-market-crash/)).

Delta India's fee rule changes two of the imported habits. The option fee is **0.01 % of notional capped at 3.5 % of premium, plus 18 % GST**, so a leg whose premium exceeds 0.286 % of notional pays 1.18 % of premium and a far wing below that threshold pays the full **4.13 %**; a leg that expires worthless pays no settlement fee ([Delta Exchange fees](https://www.delta.exchange/fees); [Delta fee blog](https://www.delta.exchange/blog/how-to-minimize-your-crypto-trading-fees-and-keep-more-profit)). That favours holding short legs to expiry over closing at 50 %, which doubles fee events, and it means a four-leg condor with cheap wings surrenders a meaningful share of a 3-vol-point premium before slippage; Deribit's 12.5 % cap is worse still ([Deribit](https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/25944746248989)). Sizing is the other imported rule to adjust: practitioner guidance has moved from "about 30 % of capital in strangles" in 2016 SPY backtests to **3-7 % of buying power per undefined-risk position** in 2026, and in crypto defined-risk structures are the only ones whose worst case is known in advance ([tastylive allocation study](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/allocating-capital-for-strangles-07-18-2016)).

## Carry, mean reversion, seasonality, factors, on-chain and macro: paper edges that Delta's fees or structure remove

Carry is a genuine premium in every traditional asset class, which is why its crypto version attracts attention. Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt find carry predicts returns in equities, bonds, currencies, commodities, credit and options with drafts reporting a Sharpe of about 0.7-0.8 per asset class; the G10 FX carry trade earned a Sharpe of 0.78 over 1976-2013 with "large" drawdowns and negative skew; and the Treasury cash-futures basis trade reached roughly **$1 trillion of short futures notional by end-2023 and unwound violently in March 2020 when hedge funds net sold about $173 billion of Treasuries** ([Koijen et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:127:y:2018:i:2:p:197-225); [Daniel-Hodrick-Lu](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/6378/Daniel.Hodrick.Lu.Carry%20Trade.Critical%20Finance%20Review.2017.pdf); [Federal Reserve FEDS Note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/quantifying-treasury-cash-futures-basis-trades-20240308.html)). The BIS paper on crypto carry says it averages "above 10 % annually", peaks above 40 %, is driven by trend-chasing leveraged small investors and scarce arbitrage capital, and **predicts crashes** ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)). Independent measurements corroborate the shape: mean implied quarterly carry on Binance of **8.7 % (BTC) and 8.2 % (ETH) over 2021-2025** with a short-leg liquidation incidence of 16 % at 5x and 47 % at 10x; funding of 7.8-9.2 % a year with **16-18 % of days negative** and a 176-day positive streak; CME basis of 15-25 % at the February and November 2024 peaks, below 5 % by February 2025, and **about 5 % against a 4.5 % risk-free rate by April 2026**, when CME open interest hit a 14-month low ([BSIC](https://bsic.it/?p=19265); [Ethena](https://docs.ethena.fi/solution-overview/risks/funding-risk); [CF Benchmarks](https://www.cfbenchmarks.com/blog/revisiting-the-bitcoin-basis-how-momentum-sentiment-impact-the-structural-drivers-of-basis-activity); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/396722/cme-bitcoin-futures-activity-slumps-to-14-month-low-as-basis-trade-unwind-drains-institutional-demand)). Our funding download confirms the levels on Delta: mean BTC funding implied an annual long cost of 21.2 % in 2021, 12.2 % in 2024, 8.1 % in 2025 and 8.0 % in 2026 ([Verification, data section](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). On Delta the trade has a structural problem on top of the economic one: there is no spot BTC leg, so a Delta-only carry must be short perp against a long options synthetic, paying two option fees plus a perp round trip of 0.047 % at maker or 0.118 % at taker, and it still eats the negative-funding streaks and the margin spike on the short leg. It is viable in 15-25 % funding regimes and roughly zero at 2025-26 levels; the honest playbook is a funding-threshold alert, not a standing strategy. Funding as a directional signal fares worse: K33's "negative 30-day funding precedes a +79 % average 90-day return" rests on six events, a 2022-2026 z-score contrarian earned **+6.3 bp per trade at 4 bp cost and turned negative in 2025**, and at 24-hour horizons extreme positive funding behaved as momentum (price up 71 % of the time), so against Delta's 11.8 bp taker round trip the naive signal is a guaranteed loss ([The Block on K33](https://www.theblock.co/post/315831/perp-signal-bitcoin-bottom-bullish-year-end-k33); [askrubyai](https://askrubyai.github.io/blog/posts/2026-02-26-funding-regime-filter/index.html)).

Mean reversion is where our control test earned its keep. The literature says short-horizon reversal is real and robustly detectable but belongs to liquidity providers: **90 % of 183 Binance pairs show significant 15-minute reversal against 2.7 % of US stocks, with a gross edge peaking near 1.3 bp per trade against a 5 bp round-trip cost**; pairs trading on 26 Binance coins "underperform[ed] classical benchmarks", with the daily distance method at −0.07 % a month ([Kitron and Wengrowicz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21888); [Fil and Kristoufek](https://doaj.org/article/8fbfa5ffeaae44a5a4c635f23f5ca05c)). Our hourly BTC test found the same shape at a longer horizon. Going long for 24 hours after a 24-hour drop of more than 3 % had a **59 % hit rate and +0.30 % gross per trade over 2019-2026 (against an unconditional 52 % and +0.15 %), but after 0.14 % round-trip costs and a day of funding the net Sharpe was 0.31 over the full window and −0.07 in the India era**; shorting after a +3 % rise lost money in 6 of 8 years (−0.21 % gross per trade), and the 48-hour z-score version lost on both sides in every window ([Verification, Part C](proven-strategies-crypto-notes/empirical_verification_delta.md)). This is exactly the standard the trend and option results had to clear: a plausible rule with a 53-59 % hit rate that loses after costs. The long-after-drop half is a candidate to monitor at maker fees, not a strategy to trade at taker.

Seasonality splits cleanly into a robust half and a folklore half. Volatility and volume seasonality is documented in a large-sample study and has strengthened: BTC and ETH volatility peaks around **16:00 UTC (21:30 IST) and bottoms near 05:00 UTC (10:30 IST)**, spikes at the 00:00 and 16:00 funding timestamps, bursts in the first minute of every hour, and runs 0.3-0.5 log-points lower on Saturdays; Kaiko adds that the weekend share of BTC volume fell from about a quarter in 2018-2021 to 13 % in early 2024 and that US-hours volume reached 46 % ([Hansen, Kim and Kimbrough](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.12142); [The Block on Kaiko](https://www.theblock.co/post/279353/bitcoin-weekend-trading)). Return seasonality is contested: a Monday effect in 2013-2017 data, Friday and 22:00-23:00 UTC effects in a 2015-2022 Gemini sample with an indicative Sharpe of 1.58 and no costs, against a 15-million-observation seven-exchange study that found "no consistent or persistent patterns" and concluded the market is weak-form efficient, and no post-2022 out-of-sample test anywhere ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/intraday-seasonality-in-bitcoin); [Baur et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finlet:v:31:y:2019:i:c:p:78-92)). Our own hourly tables are consistent with the volatility half: the worst seller window is 17:30-19:30 IST as the US session opens and the only non-losing one is 14:30-16:30 IST (`docs/research/backtest-daily-structures.md`). The product use is timing (when to expect gamma, when spreads widen, when not to rest orders), not direction.

Equity-style factors mostly do not transfer, and what does is not tradeable on Delta. Size and two-week momentum are the only cross-sectional premia that survive multiple teams and the post-2020 subsample, but **size lives in micro-caps and momentum needs rotation of up to 85 % per week with alphas falling 26-53 % after costs**; "value" is a price-to-new-addresses ratio on altcoins; low-volatility and betting-against-beta evidence is contradictory, with the largest liquid-coin study finding highest-idiosyncratic-risk coins at −9.4 % a year against +80.6 % for the lowest but requiring 50 % borrowing with a potential −84 % loss to exploit; and no credible quality factor exists ([Borri et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4); [Fieberg et al.](https://open.icm.edu.pl/items/2350d455-72e4-4845-a442-9c4024be7fb6/full); [University of Sydney](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2023/11/01/huge-crypto-study-finds-high-risk-doesnt-equal-reward.html)). The transferable lesson is negative: there is no priced reward for carrying high-beta or high-idiosyncratic-volatility exposure in crypto, so leverage-for-yield has no factor behind it. On-chain and flow signals sort the same way. Stablecoin net inflow to exchanges predicts BTC and ETH returns at 1-6 hour horizons and new-user growth explains about 8 % of return variation with no post-announcement drift, but **ETF flows correlate 0.54 with the prior five sessions and 0.03 with the next five**, MVRV, SOPR, Puell and miner reserves have no peer-reviewed predictive test, and stock-to-flow, Metcalfe, sentiment and technical measures all fail out of sample in the one paper that checked ([Chi, Chu and Hao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327); [Cointelegraph on DWF Labs](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-etf-flows-follow-price-trends-dwf-labs); [Shelton](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jjrfmx/v17y2024i10p443-d1490211.html)). Macro events are volatility events, not drifts: the equity pre-FOMC drift of about 49 bp per meeting has faded and has no crypto analogue, while **a 1 percentage-point CPI upside surprise moved BTC about −5.5 % in the first hour over 2020-2026 (R² about 25 %, peaking at 52 % in 2022-23)**, a reaction that cannot be positioned for but argues for reducing short gamma into releases ([Liberty Street Economics](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2012/07/the-puzzling-pre-fomc-announcement-drift/); [Block Scholes](https://www.blockscholes.com/institutional-research/is-bitcoin-showing-greater-sensitivity-to-us-cpi-releases-again); [Pinchuk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10117)).

The risk rules close the loop. Kelly theory is settled: full Kelly maximises long-run growth but "it is possible, through bad scenarios, to lose most of one's wealth", and fractional Kelly is the standard response to estimation error and fat tails ([MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)). No crypto test of half-Kelly or "1-2 % risk per trade" exists; those are conventions. What does have crypto numbers is volatility scaling (drawdown cut from 77 % to 12 % at a 20 % target in the Quantpedia composite; "left tails of Bitcoin more benign than those of the S&P 500" once scaled, per Man AHL), a simple strategy-level stop-loss that "significantly improves the Sharpe ratios" in liquid low-volatility crypto portfolios, and the fat-tail facts to size against: a post-2020 coin-market drawdown of −77.6 % and **jumps in 18.8 % of BTC five-minute intervals against about 5 % for US stocks** ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/dual-momentum-allocation-between-physical-gold-and-bitcoin-digital-gold/?a=6080); [Man AHL](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend); [Kaya and Mostowfi](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/handle/11475/23853?mode=full); [Borri et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)). The evidence-consistent stack for a Delta options desk is defined-risk structures by default, size by realised or implied volatility to a fixed risk budget, total exposure at half Kelly or less because edge estimates from six years of regime data are noisy, and a strategy-level stop rather than leg stops that fight gamma.

## Conclusion

The research reorders the playbook priorities in `docs/research/strategy-playbooks.md`. That document called carry "the best-evidenced group" and proposed premium harvest, an expiry-week credit spread and trend-with-defined-risk as the first three. The evidence now says trend is the only family with a first-party, after-cost, multi-plateau confirmation; premium harvest is a shrinking premium with no crypto backtest and a fee rule that penalises the imported "manage at 50 %" habit; and expiry-day selling survives only as a narrow final-hours scalp. The first playbook to ship should therefore be trend following, long/flat, with the lookback drawn from the 40-70 or 100-150 day plateaus, volatility-sized, carrying its own yearly track record (including the losing 2022 and 2025 columns) on the card, and offered with a perp expression as the base case and a call-spread or put-spread expression as the gap-protected variant whose lower expected return is stated. Premium harvest should ship second and only after the backtest engine has run 16-20 delta condors at 30-45 DTE on the recorded weekly and monthly chains with the 0.01 % / 3.5 % / GST rule, hold-to-expiry versus 50 % management compared, and the rich wing chosen by risk reversal rather than by equity habit. Hero-or-zero, naked daily strangles, symmetric mean reversion, funding contrarianism, return seasonality and on-chain cycle indicators should be absent from the product as playbooks; several belong in the product as warnings.

Two things changed in understanding. First, on Delta the binding constraint on a directional perpetual strategy is funding, not fees: 9-12 CAGR points a year on the long side against 1-2 for fees, which makes the options expression of trend, and dated or synthetic alternatives to a standing perp long, worth testing before the trend playbook is finalised. Second, the hit rate is the wrong statistic for every options family here: 88-100 % winning days for the seller and 59 % for the mean-reversion long both coexisted with negative expectancy, and the product's track-record cards should lead with worst day as a multiple of average credit and with the yearly columns, not with win rate. The unrun tests that would most change these verdicts are vol-targeted trend on our own data, trend expressed through option spreads, weekly and monthly premium selling on `chain_eod`, ETH and XAUT options, a bootstrap on the trend Sharpe differences, and a longer daily-option sample that contains at least one large down day.
