# Options premium-selling and volatility strategies: the variance risk premium in equities and in crypto (BTC/ETH)

Researched 8 November 2026. Confidence tags used throughout: **[PR]** peer-reviewed or exchange/index-provider methodology document, **[PF]** practitioner firm (exchange research desk, data vendor, asset manager, index provider marketing), **[BL]** blog, forum, aggregator, anonymous or self-published. Where only an abstract or a second-hand summary was visible this is stated. Every number carries its sample period, asset, delta/DTE and cost treatment where the source gave them; "costs: not stated" means the source was silent.

## Key question 1: Long-run excess return and Sharpe of systematic index put-writing and buy-write in equities, and whether it survived 2018, 2020 and 2022

### Takeaway
Over 1986-2015 the Cboe PUT index earned roughly the same compound return as the S&P 500 total-return index (10.1% vs 9.8%) at two-thirds of the volatility (10.1% vs 15.3%), for a gross Sharpe of 0.67 vs 0.47, with a worse skew (-2.09 vs -0.79) and a 32.7% max drawdown vs 50.9%; the premium survived the 2018, 2020 and 2022 drawdowns (PUT fell about 25% in Feb-Mar 2020 vs the S&P 500's roughly 34%), but one 2026 self-published analysis finds the writing indices lagged buy-and-hold by 4.5-6.2%/yr over 2020-2026, so the post-2020 record is weaker than the long-run one.

### Inferences
- The equity put-write premium is well documented over 30-plus years and is an index-level, fully collateralised, ATM, monthly, gross-of-cost result; its edge is lower volatility and drawdown rather than higher return. The post-2020 bull run shrank the relative return, which is expected when the underlying rallies hard (short upside convexity).
- Every blow-up episode (2018, 2020) involved leverage or un-collateralised exposure, not the fully collateralised index construction; PUT itself had drawdowns of 25-33% and recovered.
- The equity VRP is positive on about 86% of days (one source), so a long-run short-premium rule in equities expects roughly one losing window in seven.

### Gaps
- The Cboe PUT factsheet PDF could not be text-extracted in this environment (no PDF library), so the current annualised return, Sharpe and calendar-year 2018/2020/2022 figures from Cboe itself are not quoted; the 2020 and 2022 drawdowns above are from a third-party data site.
- Carr-Wu exact mean log variance risk premia and Sharpe ratios were not visible (abstract only).
- No source gave a CNDR or BFLY annualised return since 2015.

## Key question 2: Is the BTC implied-minus-realised volatility spread persistently positive? Average and share of months positive

### Takeaway
Yes in every study found, but the size has collapsed: Almeida et al. (Deribit, July 2017-Dec 2022) find an annualised variance risk premium of 0.14 (implied variance 0.72 vs realised 0.58), far above the S&P 500's roughly 0.02; a 2026 self-published DVOL study (Mar 2021-Jun 2026) finds BTC implied minus forward 30-day realised averaged +8.9 vol points, positive on 74% of days (ETH +6.1 vol points, 67%), but the premium fell from 13.2 vol points in the early window to 3.4 in the late window (ETH 11.4 to 0.2) and the short-variance harvest Sharpe fell from 2.82 (2021-23) to 0.27 (2024-26).

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] Almeida, Grith, Miftachov and Wang, "Risk Premia in the Bitcoin Market" (arXiv 2410.15195, 2024), Deribit daily transaction-level options July 2017-Dec 2022, 27-day options, no fees:** BVRP = risk-neutral variance minus physical variance = 0.14 overall (sigma2_Q 0.72, sigma2_P 0.58, annualised variance units); 0.12 in the high-volatility cluster (0.86 vs 0.74), 0.17 in the low-volatility cluster (0.46 vs 0.29); "much higher than that of the S&P 500 Index", citing Bollerslev et al. (2009) for an S&P 500 VRP of about 2%; the sign flips occasionally and realised variance exceeded BVIX2 between 15 March and 8 April 2020 when the VRP fell below -2; the share of positive days is not reported; the pricing kernel is hump-shaped, and returns between +20% and +60% account for 38.66% of the overall Bitcoin premium (52.30% in the low-vol regime), i.e. a large call-side premium — [arXiv 2410.15195v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15195v2); [Harbourfront Quant summary](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/return-and-variance-risk-premia-in)
- **[PR] Alexander & Imeraj, "The Bitcoin VIX and its Variance Risk Premium" (Journal of Alternative Investments, 2021), Deribit, March 2019-March 2020, constant maturities 10-90 days, realised variance at 15-minute, hourly and daily sampling:** first study of bitcoin VRP and the term structure of fair-value variance swap rates; the index is streamed by CryptoCompare as BVIN; the authors' 2020 blog states the VRP is typically negative in their sign convention (realised minus implied), i.e. investors pay for variance insurance; full text not accessible (Sussex repository restricted) — [SSRN author page](https://papers.ssrn.com/author=3583718); [Sussex record](https://sro.sussex.ac.uk/id/eprint/91094/); [Medium post by the authors](https://medium.com/@cryptomarketrisk/bitcoin-vix-signals-red-alert-for-variance-swaps-8a6583d2f0db)
- **[PR, abstract only] A bitcoin options market paper (title not confirmed) reports the bitcoin volatility risk premium is "consistently negative, much larger in magnitude than that of S&P 500 options", attributed to market makers finding inventory risk harder to hedge and charging more — surfaced in search snippets alongside [Alexander, Deng, Feng, Wan, "Net Buying Pressure and the Information in Bitcoin Option Trades" (arXiv 2109.02776)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02776), whose abstract supports a limits-to-arbitrage (constrained market-maker supply) story on Deribit; the numeric VRP claim could not be tied to a specific paper
- **[PR] Hoang & Baur (2020, Journal of Futures Markets), Deribit options:** implied volatility combined with model forecasts gives the best realised-vol forecasts; this is a forecasting paper and does not estimate the VRP — [vLex record](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/forecasting-bitcoin-volatility-evidence-855595395)
- **[PR] Winkel & Haerdle (2023, Risks):** Deribit-based pricing kernels and risk premia (abstract only) — [HU Berlin edoc](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/18452/27204)
- **[BL] Bryan Vine, Alpha Research Paper 1 (16 June 2026), Deribit DVOL and index 12-hour bars 24 Mar 2021-16 Jun 2026 (3,822 bars), VRP = (DVOL/100)2 minus forward 30-day realised variance, close-to-close RV:** BTC +8.90 vol points, positive 74% of days, Newey-West t 5.18, correlation to spot +0.12; ETH +6.06 vol points, 67% positive, t 1.89; equity (VIX vs SPX) +4.09, 86%; compression: BTC 13.2 to 3.4 vol points, ETH 11.4 to 0.2; short-variance-swap harvest Sharpe (close-to-close RV) 1.53 BTC / 1.02 ETH, but 0.42 / 0.14 with Parkinson RV and -0.04 / -0.10 with Garman-Klass; by period BTC 2.82 (2021-23) vs 0.27 (2024-26), ETH 1.94 vs -0.13; deflated Sharpe 0.88 / 0.55; default round-trip cost 2 vol points, current ATM Deribit half-spread about 2.7-3.9% of premium; author says the premium has been "arbitraged toward zero in our live window" — [Alpha Research Paper 1](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper1.html)
- **[PF] Amberdata exposes a "DVOL Variance Premium" endpoint (DVOL2 minus realised variance)** — [Amberdata docs](https://docs.amberdata.io/docs/iv-dvol)
- **[PF] Deribit DVOL definition:** 30-day forward-looking annualised implied volatility built from the two expiries bracketing 30 days; BTC DVOL futures launched March 2023 — [Deribit Insights](https://insights.deribit.com/?p=1813)
- **[PF] Glassnode weekly notes (2025-2026, via aggregators):** at one point 1-month realised 27% vs implied 35% ("VRP near recent highs"); in another window realised rose from 27% to 41% while implied fell toward 41% and the gap "had largely disappeared"; June 2026: implied above 40% vs realised near 35%; Glassnode noted that falling implied and realised vol "supported carry for volatility sellers" — [KuCoin flash on Glassnode](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-options-traders-remain-cautious-amid-volatility-compression); [Glassnode options premium metrics](https://insights.glassnode.com/options-premiums-metrics)
- **[PF] Deribit desk commentary:** "selling crypto volatility came of age in 2022 and afforded stellar returns", and the vol market "behaved relatively well" through UST, 3AC and FTX — [Deribit Insights, Vol commentary week 45 2022](https://insights.deribit.com/industry/vol-commentary-week-45-2022/)

### Inferences
- The BTC VRP is positive on average in every sample found (2017-2022 peer-reviewed; 2021-2026 self-published), with a positive-day share (74%) lower than equities (86%); this means more frequent losing windows for crypto sellers than for equity sellers.
- The 2024-2026 compression (to about 3 vol points for BTC, near zero for ETH) is the single most important finding for product design: a naive "sell because implied exceeds realised" rule had little edge in the most recent two years, and the result is sensitive to the realised-vol estimator (close-to-close flatters it; range-based estimators remove most of the edge).
- Almeida et al.'s finding that a large share of the premium sits in the +20% to +60% return region supports selling calls (not only puts) in crypto, which is the opposite of the equity norm.

### Gaps
- No source reported the share of *months* with positive BTC implied-minus-realised; only the share of days (74%) from one self-published source.
- The Alexander & Imeraj full text was not accessible; their numeric VRP estimates are not quoted.
- No 2023-2026 SSRN paper measuring returns to selling bitcoin variance was found; the only trading-rule evidence is practitioner or self-published.

## Key question 4: Which delta/DTE/IV-rank filters improved results, and which rules are folklore

### Takeaway
Documented: 45 DTE with 16-20 delta (tastylive, SPY, 2005-2017), higher deltas when IV rank is high, IV rank above 50 improving win rate and credit (tastylive, 2014), shorter DTE and lower delta cutting drawdown at the cost of return (Option Alpha, SPY, 10 DTE/15 delta: DD 13% vs 37%). Contested: "close at 50% / 21 DTE" raises P/L per day and win rate in tastylive's studies but an independent SPX 2001-2020 test (no costs) found a passive 30 DTE entry / 5 DTE exit beat it (CAGR 5.46% vs 4.44%, Sharpe 0.67 vs 0.52). Folklore with no backtest found: "stop at 2x credit" as a universal rule, and any crypto-specific IV-rank threshold.

### Cited Findings
- **[PF] tastylive Mega Strangle Study (May 2014), 1-SD 45-DTE strangles, five underlyings, 314 trades, costs not stated:** high-IV entries: total P/L $3,710 managing at 50% of max profit, $6,535 holding to expiry; win rate 93% (50% management) and 89% (hold); low-IV trades "still successful but not as attractive" — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/mega-strangle-study-05-12-2014)
- **[PF] tastylive Entry Level IV Rank (June 2014):** IV rank above 25 acceptable; above 50 gives higher win rate, higher credit and higher profit per trade — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/entry-level-iv-rank-06-03-2014)
- **[PF] tastylive Scaling Deltas in High IV (Sep 2018), SPY 2005-2017, 45 DTE, 16/20/30 delta:** scaling deltas higher when IVR is higher "provides substantial increases in P/L"; in low IVR P/L is flat across deltas — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/scaling-deltas-in-high-iv-09-28-2018)
- **[PF] tastylive Strangles with Varying Deltas (Nov 2016):** higher delta gives greater average P/L but lower win rate; theoretical success rates about 68% (16 delta) and 55% (30 delta), with realised 30-delta success above 55% — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/strangles-with-varying-deltas-11-22-2016); [tastylive, Selling options yields consistent success](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/selling-options-yields-consistent-success)
- **[PF] tastylive 2025:** "sweet spot appears to be in the 16-20 delta range" — [tastylive Options Jive, May 2025](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/can-delta-help-you-time-profits-in-a-strangle-05-06-2025)
- **[PF] tastylive Short Premium in Low IV (Aug 2017), SPY:** short strangles profitable in all IV environments; adding 10-delta wings in low IVR hurt performance more than running naked — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/short-premium-in-low-iv-the-worst-of-the-best-08-31-2017)
- **[PF] tastylive Managing Strangles (Aug 2016), SPY 1-SD 45 DTE daily entries:** holding to expiry gave higher average P/L per trade but managing at 50% gave more P/L per day; managing winners "didn't stop the largest losses" but turned about half of small losers into winners — [tastylive](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/managing-strangles-performance-08-05-2016); [tastylive, Strangles visualized](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/strangles-visualized-performance-07-19-2016)
- **[PF] tastylive Managing Losers in Risk-Defined Strategies (Oct 2016), 45 DTE wide iron condor (16-delta short, 5-delta wings):** closing before 21 DTE beat managing losers — [tastylive](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/managing-losers-in-risk-defined-strategies-10-24-2016)
- **[BL] SteadyOptions / ORATS test (Jesse), SPX 16-delta strangle, 2001-May 2020, end-of-day data, no slippage or commissions, 1x notional:** 45 DTE entry with exit at 50% of credit or 21 DTE: CAGR 4.44%, vol 8.62%, Sharpe 0.52; 30 DTE entry / 5 DTE exit, no management: CAGR 5.46%, vol 8.19%, Sharpe 0.67; 25/16-delta slightly bullish version: CAGR 6.30%, Sharpe 0.63; author: results "favor the second approach", "difficult to differentiate luck from skill" — [SteadyOptions](https://steadyoptions.com/articles/does-“managing-winners”-add-value-to-short-strangles-r618/)
- **[BL/PF] Option Alpha SPY short strangle backtest (published 2017, updated 2022), period not stated, commissions not stated, no stops or targets:** 30 DTE 20-delta: win rate 79.6%, max DD 37%, total return 145%, CAGR 9.41%, Sharpe 0.62; 10 DTE 15-delta: win rate 82%, max DD 13%, total return 125%, CAGR 8.48%; conclusion: shorter DTE collected more premium but was not more profitable, equity curve smoother — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/win-rates-when-selling-options-89970.html); Option Alpha separately reported a strangle backtest where the no-stop version returned 6,349% and a 3x stop-loss version returned less — [Option Alpha, Stop loss strategies](https://optionalpha.com/podcast/stop-loss-strategies-for-options)
- **[BL] Spintwig via Early Retirement Now (2020), SPY put writing Feb 2018-June 2020, 2-2.5x leverage:** results section not retrievable in the fetched text — [Early Retirement Now part 5](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2020/06/17/passive-income-through-option-writing-part-5)
- **[PR] Pepperdine working paper:** selling SPY strangles "generally profitable across a variety of widths" with unlimited-loss caveat (abstract only) — [Pepperdine Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.pepperdine.edu/graziadiowps/25)
- **[PR] Bakshi & Kapadia:** delta-hedged shortfall is smaller for options away from the money, which is the academic basis for selling OTM rather than ATM — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i2p527-566.html)

### Inferences
- Rules with documented support (equities, SPY/SPX, 2001-2020, mostly gross of costs): 30-45 DTE, 16-20 delta (or higher delta only when IV rank is high), entering regardless of IV rank on the index but demanding IV rank above 50 on single names, no wings in low IV.
- "Manage at 50% / 21 DTE" is supported on P/L-per-day and win-rate metrics by the vendor whose brand it is, and contradicted on CAGR/Sharpe by an independent test; it is a risk-smoothing rule, not a return-enhancing one.
- "Stop at 2x credit" has no backtest in the sources found; the one stop-loss result found (3x stop) reduced total return. Treat stop rules as drawdown controls to be tested, not proven.
- None of these studies is on crypto; the regime difference (positive-skew episodes, 24/7 gaps, higher kurtosis) means equity filter thresholds should be re-derived from crypto data before being presented in-app.

### Gaps
- No ORATS or Project Option study with exact numbers was retrieved; the ORATS result is second-hand via SteadyOptions.
- No source tested "stop at 2x credit" or "IV percentile" filters explicitly.
- No crypto study of IV-rank filters exists in the sources found.

## Key question 5: How fees matter when premiums are small, and any source discussing fee drag on crypto short premium

### Takeaway
Deribit charges 0.03% of the underlying index per side, capped at 12.5% of the option price, the loosest cap among major venues; Delta Exchange India's current schedule (as listed by TradingView and a Delta promotion) is 0.01% of notional capped at 3.5% of premium, with 18% GST on fees; the only source that models fee drag on crypto vol selling assumes 2 vol points round-trip and finds the 2024-2026 harvest roughly flat after costs. For a far-OTM wing whose premium is 0.1-0.2% of notional, the Deribit fee reaches its 12.5% cap on every leg, so a four-leg condor can surrender a quarter or more of its net credit to fees before slippage.

### Cited Findings
- **[PF] Deribit options fee:** 0.03% of the underlying (index price) for makers and takers, capped at 12.5% of the option price — [Deribit support](https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/25944746248989); third-party comparison: on a $200 premium at $100,000 notional Deribit charges $25 vs $8.26 at Delta Exchange and $14 at OKX; Delta caps at 3.5%, OKX at 7% — [Spark.money comparison](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-options-exchange-comparison)
- **[PF] Delta Exchange (global help centre):** 0.03% of notional capped at 12.5% of premium; same calculation for sellers; settlement fee 0.03% capped at 12.5% charged as taker; no fee when an option expires worthless; worked example 0.3 BTC at $26,200 spot with $15 premium: notional fee $2.358 vs cap $0.5625, lower applies — [Delta Exchange help](https://deltaexchange.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/80001140285-fees-on-options-and-futures-trading)
- **[PF] Delta Exchange India:** options fee 0.010% of notional (reduced from 0.03%), premium cap 3.5% (reduced from 10%) per a Delta holiday offer page — [Delta Exchange India X-Mas offer](https://deltaexchangeindia.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/80001190846-x-mas-offer); TradingView broker page lists "Options: 0.01% of notional traded value capped at 3.5% of Options Premium" — [TradingView, Delta Exchange vs Share.Market](https://in.tradingview.com/brokers/compare/DeltaExchange-vs-Share_Market/?country=IN); Delta India fee article confirms the 3.5% cap — [Delta Exchange India help](https://deltaexchangeindia.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/80001177864-fees-on-options-and-futures-trading); 18% GST applies on trading fees on Indian platforms — [Delta Exchange blog](https://www.delta.exchange/blog/how-to-minimize-your-crypto-trading-fees-and-keep-more-profit)
- **[PF] Real Delta India option fee in the project's own research:** 0.01% / cap 3.5% / GST, already recorded in HapieCoin's paper-timing analysis (GAPS #129) and found to make every daily structure lose after fees except the 14:00-16:00 IST window — project memory note (docs/research/paper-timing-analysis.md); verify against the repo before citing externally
- **[BL] Fee drag modelled on crypto vol selling:** 2 vol points round-trip default, 0-5 sweep; ATM Deribit half-spread about 2.7-3.9% of premium (about 2.7 vol points round-trip); OTM wings cost more; the 2024-2026 harvest is flat after this cost — [Alpha Research Paper 1](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper1.html)
- **[BL] CryptoVol covered-call backtest:** mid fills; author estimates live execution lowers monthly 50-delta returns by 2-4pp over two years — [CryptoVol](https://cryptovol.vercel.app/blog/btc-covered-call-backtest)
- **[PR] Quantpedia equity VRP backtest** includes bid-ask (sell at bid, buy hedge at ask) but no commissions — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/volatility-risk-premium-effect/)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic (mine, from the schedules above): a Deribit leg hits its 12.5% cap whenever the premium is below 0.24% of the index (0.03/0.125); a 16-delta 30-day BTC option at 50% IV has premium of roughly 0.5-1% of spot (approximation), so the fee is about 3-6% of premium per leg, but 5-delta wings (premium often below 0.2% of spot) pay the full 12.5% on open and again on close if not left to expire. Delta India's cap of 3.5% plus 18% GST gives a maximum of about 4.1% of premium per leg, and a leg that expires worthless pays no fee at Delta, which favours holding short legs to expiry over closing at 50%.
- The "close at 50% of max profit" rule roughly doubles the number of fee events per year; in crypto, where the 2024-2026 premium was only about 3 vol points, management rules imported from equities can turn a positive gross edge into a negative net one.

### Gaps
- No research-firm or academic source quantifies fee drag on crypto short-premium strategies at the 12.5% or 3.5% caps; the only modelled figure is a blog's vol-point assumption.
- The Deribit fee page content was only seen in a cached snippet; the standing Delta India rate (0.01% vs promotional) was not confirmed from Delta India's own fee article text.

## Key question 6: Position-sizing and margin rules practitioners cite to survive the tails

### Takeaway
Documented (tastylive, SPY strangles, 2005-2016): 25-35% of capital in strangle buying-power reduction beat buy-and-hold with lower return volatility, 50% caused margin problems; newer guidance is 3-7% of buying power per undefined-risk position, and buying power reduction grows about 23% through a trade (12-15% if closed at 21 DTE); crypto-specific sizing evidence is anecdotal (Deribit's insurance fund fell about half in three days in March 2020 and the exchange injected 500 BTC to avoid socialised losses), so defined-risk structures and sub-10% buying-power use per position are the only rules with any backing.

### Cited Findings
- **[PF] tastylive Allocating Capital for Strangles (July 2016), SPY 1-SD 45 DTE vs 100% SPY:** 20/25/30/50% allocations tested; 50% "might cause margin issues"; about 30% optimal and never triggered a margin call — [tastylive](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/allocating-capital-for-strangles-07-18-2016)
- **[PF] tastylive Portfolio Allocation for Strangles parts 1-2 (Sep-Oct 2016):** managing at 50% beat buy-and-hold at nearly every allocation, 35% best; managing one month before expiry, 25%+ beat buy-and-hold with lower volatility of returns; a portfolio leveraged 100% in strangles "highly unlikely to survive the drawdowns"; sizing formula = account x target allocation / BPR per contract, or one-third of available buying power — [tastylive part 1](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/portfolio-allocation-for-strangles-part-1-09-26-2016); [tastylive part 2](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/portfolio-allocation-for-strangles-part-2-10-03-2016); [tastylive, Mechanical strategies](https://tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/mechanical-strategies-trading-a-strangle-portfolio-05-25-2016)
- **[PF] tastylive 2024-2026:** buying power reduction rises about 23% to expiration, 12-15% if managed at 21 DTE; 2026 session suggests 3-7% of buying power per undefined-risk position — [tastylive, Evolution of BPR](https://tastylive.com/shows/options-jive/episodes/evolution-of-bpr-throughout-a-trade-06-03-2024); [Pulse.bot digest of a 2026 tastylive session](https://www.pulse.bot/options-derivatives/video/calculated-risk-dr-jim-schultz-undefined-risk-options-position-sizing-strangle-short-put-management-fe7eecf5-2b16-42f2-9262-b4133f8559bf/)

### Inferences
- The practitioner consensus has tightened over a decade from "30% of capital in strangles" (2016 SPY backtests) to "3-7% of buying power per undefined-risk position" (2026), which is consistent with the equity blow-ups of 2018 and 2020 having happened in between.
- In crypto the exchange itself is the tail: insurance-fund depletion, quote withdrawal and ADL mean a short-premium position can be closed at a price unrelated to the model; defined-risk (condor, credit spread) is the only structure whose worst case is known in advance, at the cost of the wing's fee and premium.
- No source quantifies half-Kelly or a 1-2% risk rule for options selling with a backtest; these are generic money-management heuristics, not findings.

- No DVOL reading for 5 Aug 2024 was found (only Volmex BVIV).

## Key question 7 (part 5 of the brief): Term structure, skew and IV rank in crypto vs equities, and which side to sell

### Takeaway
Equity index options carry a persistent put premium and a large negative spot-vol correlation; bitcoin's smile was roughly symmetric before March 2020, turned to a flatter-than-equity put skew after it, and frequently flips to call-rich in bull phases (BTC spot-IV correlation was -0.42 Aug 2019-Nov 2020, +0.74 over the following five months, 0.08 Jul-Nov 2022); the term structure cycles between backwardation in stress and contango in calm; the peer-reviewed pricing-kernel evidence puts 39-52% of the bitcoin premium in the +20% to +60% return region, so the call side carries premium in crypto in a way it does not in equities.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] Alexander & Imeraj, "Delta hedging bitcoin options with a smile" (Quantitative Finance 2023):** BTC price vs 30-day ATM IV correlation about -0.42 (Aug 2019-Nov 2020), then +0.74 over the next five months, 0.08 (Jul-Nov 2022); equity correlation "typically large and negative"; before 12 March 2020 the BTC smile was roughly symmetric (ATM about 50%, 0.7 and 1.3 moneyness about 75%); after it a pronounced negative skew appeared, flatter than typical equity skew; term structure cycles between backwardation (stress) and contango (calm) — [Harbourfront summary](https://harbourfrontquant.substack.com/p/how-bitcoin-options-compare-to-equity)
- **[PR] Almeida et al. (2024):** right slope of the IV curve steeper in the low-vol regime; 38.66% of the BTC premium from +20% to +60% returns overall, 52.30% in the low-vol regime — [arXiv 2410.15195v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15195v2)
- **[PF] Deribit Insights on skew:** equity investors structurally own equities so they buy puts, giving a persistent put premium; in bitcoin, covered-call selling by coin holders has made skew "significantly less positive" and it "goes negative frequently" (put-minus-call convention), while "given the parabolic advance" positive (call-rich) skew "would seem logical" — [Deribit Insights](https://insights.deribit.com/?p=1264); [Deribit, Explaining skew at $36,000](https://insights.deribit.com/market-research/explaining-skew-when-considering-btc-at-36000-usd/)
- **[PF] K33 (Dec 2021, Jan 2022):** before the 19 May and 4 Dec 2021 crashes the 6-month 25-delta skew flipped from negative (call-rich) to positive (put-rich); after the May flip BTC kept falling for two months — [K33](https://k33.com/research/archive/articles/medium-term-far-out-the-money-option-flows-foreshadowing-bitcoin-sell-offs)
- **[PF] Deribit (June 2024):** calls still priced above puts post-halving — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/291110/bitcoin-options-in-line-with-previous-halvings-and-bullishly-pricing-calls-higher-than-puts-analyst)
- **[BL] CryptoVol 1-month 25-delta risk reversal, 30-day average, Jun 2024-May 2026:** near 0 to -2% mid-2024, neutral to slightly positive late 2024-Jan 2025, drifting negative through 2025, about -10% in the Feb 2026 selloff, recovered only to -5% — [CryptoVol risk reversal](https://cryptovol.vercel.app/blog/btc-risk-reversal-skew)
- **[PF] Glassnode via CryptoSlate (early 2024):** six-month skew around -6% (put preference) — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/insights/bitcoins-25-delta-skew-experiences-sharp-fluctuations-amid-correction)
- **[PF] Laevitas (late Dec 2025):** risk reversals negative across all tenors — [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/bitcoin-news-update-btc-nears-red-2025-as-options-skew-and-etf-outflows-grow)
- **[PF] Kaiko/MerQube (3 March, year not confirmed):** BTC Deribit skew 23.5% vs IBIT options 13.6% — [Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/news/kaiko-research-highlights-tail-risk-divergence-between-ibit-and-deribit-options)
- **[PF] Sign convention warning:** sources differ (put IV minus call IV on Deribit's model vs call minus put elsewhere) — [Blockmedia](https://www.blockmedia.co.kr/research-english/919328); [CryptoDataDownload](https://www.cryptodatadownload.com/blog/posts/bitcoin-option-risk-reversal-skew-binance-25delta/)
- **[BL] 2021-2026 vol regime:** DVOL and realised both compressing toward about 40-50% as the market matures — [Alpha Research Paper 1](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper1.html)
- **[PF] Deribit DVOL rule of thumb:** divide DVOL by 20 for the expected daily move — [Deribit Insights](https://insights.deribit.com/?p=1813)

### Inferences
- Because crypto skew is regime-dependent and often call-rich, the equity habit of "sell puts, they are the rich side" does not transfer; the richer side in crypto changes with the regime and the pricing-kernel evidence says calls carry real premium in low-vol bull regimes. A product should show which wing is currently rich (25-delta risk reversal) rather than defaulting to put selling.
- Positive spot-vol correlation in bull runs means a short call loses on both delta and vega at once; a short put in a crash loses on delta, vega and (in crypto) on liquidity as market makers withdraw. Defined-risk on both sides is the structural answer.
- IV rank in crypto is confounded by the secular compression of DVOL from 2021 to 2026: a fixed IV-rank lookback will read "low" for long stretches and a percentile over a shorter window is less misleading; no source tested this.

### Gaps
- No source gave a long-run average BTC 25-delta skew for a fixed tenor; only period descriptions.
- No peer-reviewed or vendor study tests "which side to sell" in crypto with returns; the inference rests on pricing-kernel decomposition and skew behaviour.
