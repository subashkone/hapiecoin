## KQ1. Which equity factors (value, size, low-vol/BAB, quality) have crypto analogues with out-of-sample support, and which failed?

### Takeaway
Size and (short-horizon) momentum are the only cross-sectional crypto premia that survive multiple research teams, the 2020-25 subsample and (for momentum) transaction costs — but size lives in micro-caps and momentum needs ~85 %/week turnover and short legs. "Value" exists only as an on-chain network-adoption ratio (not a P/E analogue), it is negative-signed (cheap-by-addresses coins outperform) and is the most contested. Low-vol/BAB evidence is contradictory across four papers; downside beta is priced positively (the opposite of a low-risk anomaly). No credible crypto "quality" factor exists in the peer-reviewed literature.

### Cited Findings

**Size and momentum (the original three-factor model)**
- Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, *Journal of Finance* 77(2), 2022: "three factors — cryptocurrency market, size, and momentum — capture the cross-sectional expected cryptocurrency returns"; "Ten cryptocurrency characteristics form successful long-short strategies" and all are explained by the 3-factor model (abstract only; the NBER PDF was not readable) — **[PR]** [EconPapers abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:2:p:1133-1177); [NBER w25882](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882.pdf)
- Search-summary detail from the same paper (not verified against the PDF): market index of 1,707 coins; momentum "performs significantly better among the larger coins"; in the small-coin group momentum earns a statistically insignificant 0.6 % per week — **[PR, summary]** [SSRN 3379131](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3379131); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2022/06/factors-investing-in-cryptocurrency)
- Updated estimates, Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu survey "Cryptocurrency as an Investable Asset Class" (arXiv 2510.14435v4, 2025), CoinGecko daily data 31 Dec 2013 – 6 Sep 2025, weekly value-weighted Q5-minus-Q1 spreads, Newey-West t-stats, **costs: not applied to the smart-beta factors**:
  - Size (CSIZE): full sample −0.023/week (t = −2.66); post-2020 −0.009 (t = −1.69) → weaker but same sign.
  - Momentum (2-week, CMOM): full sample +0.026/week (t = 3.89); post-2020 +0.021 (t = 3.70).
  - Longer momentum fails: MOM12 +0.003 (t = 0.47), MOM24 −0.009 (t = −1.50) full sample; post-2020 MOM12 +0.006 (t = 1.30), MOM24 −0.004 (t = −0.66).
  - Volatility (VOL): full −0.003 (t = −0.45); post-2020 −0.007 (t = −1.39) → not priced.
  - Stock-market beta: +0.011 (t = 1.80, 10 % only) full; post-2020 +0.007 (t = 1.17). Coin-market beta: +0.007 (t = 1.34); post-2020 +0.008 (t = 1.64) → no BAB and no positive beta premium.
  - Volume: −0.030 (t = −1.60); post-2020 −0.035 (t = −1.35) → not significant.
  - Crypto-CAPM adjusted R² on 50 test portfolios 0.046; the four-factor (market, size, momentum, value) model 0.473. — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- Fieberg, Liedtke, Metko & Zaremba, *Quantitative Finance* 23(12), 2023: replicate 34 cross-sectional crypto anomalies on >3,900 coins, 2014-2022; "past winners consistently outperform losers", effect persists across sub-periods; costs: not mentioned in abstract — **[PR]** [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v23y2023i12p1853-1869.html)
- Fieberg, Liedtke & Zaremba, "Cryptocurrency anomalies and economic constraints", *IRFA* 94 (2024), art. 103218: size and volume anomalies "originate from micro-cap coins of negligible economic importance"; momentum "prevails in larger cryptocurrencies but incurs substantial trading costs and extracts alphas largely from short positions"; momentum needs rotation "of up to 85 % per week"; after transaction costs momentum alphas fall "by 26 % to 53 %" but most portfolios stay profitable; abnormal returns occur "primarily in bull markets and fade over time" (figures from search excerpts, full text not read) — **[PR]** [ICM repository](https://open.icm.edu.pl/items/2350d455-72e4-4845-a442-9c4024be7fb6/full)
- The 2025 survey states Fieberg et al. (2024) find size and momentum robust across 20,736 research designs — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- Zhu (venue/year not confirmed), 47 crypto anomalies following Liu et al. (2022): out-of-sample anomalies differ from in-sample ones, "the disappearance of size effect and the appearance of left-tail risk effect show up out of sample" (abstract via author page only) — **[WP, unverified]** [Bohrium author page](https://www.bohrium.com/scholar/923549q5/Yifeng_Zhu)
- Mercik, Zaremba & Demir, "Crypto Factor Zoo" (working paper, date not shown): specific chosen factors have "limited temporal persistence" though broad categories are stable; combined factor portfolios beat the crypto market risk-adjusted out of sample (abstract only) — **[WP]** [ICM repository](https://open.icm.edu.pl/items/d9f44e22-0614-4db8-8585-6dcba8906bb5/full)

**Value / network factors**
- Cong, Karolyi, Tang & Zhao, "Value Premium, Network Adoption, and Factor Pricing of Crypto Assets" (WP; published as "Crypto Value, Factor Pricing, and Market Segmentation", *Management Science*, 2024/25): >4,000 crypto assets; momentum in the largest-cap group, reversals elsewhere; "strong crypto value and network adoption premia"; C-5 model (market, size, momentum, value, network) prices test portfolios "better than existing models, in-sample and out-of-sample"; published abstract says the value premium "is plausibly driven by compensation for on-chain activity risk"; disclosures: Cong advises Chainlink Labs, Karolyi consults for Avantis. Return/t-stat figures not visible (PDF blocked) — **[PR]** [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05875); [XMU abstract](https://finance.xmu.edu.cn/info/1010/33463.htm)
- Survey re-estimate of value (CVALUE = price-to-new-address ratio): full sample −0.035/week (t = −3.50), post-2020 −0.023 (t = −2.32), i.e. high-price-per-address ("expensive") coins underperform; new-user growth alone "accounts for about 8 % of the variation in cryptocurrency returns" (>10 % for the largest coins); price reacts sharply when weekly adoption data is released with "no post-announcement drift" — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- Bianchi & Babiak, "A Factor Model for Cryptocurrency Returns" (CERGE-EI WP 710, 2021; later "Mispricing and Risk Compensation in Cryptocurrency Returns", Cambridge journal): IPCA with 3 latent factors; total R² 17.2 % (predictive R² 2.9 %) vs 9.6 % (−0.02 %) for six observable factors; "liquidity, size, reversal, and both market and downside risks" drive expected returns; alphas tied to speculative demand, liquidity and reversal survive up to 8 latent factors; risk compensation increasingly reflects equity-market exposure. Sample period/coin count not in abstract — **[WP/PR]** [IDEAS wp710](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cer/papers/wp710.html); [Lancaster eprint](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/233575/1/mispricing-and-risk-compensation-in-cryptocurrency-returns.pdf)
- Borri & Shakhnov, "The Cross-Section of Cryptocurrency Returns", *Review of Asset Pricing Studies* 12(3), 2022, pp. 667-705: the "cross-section" is bitcoin prices across exchanges/currencies, not coin characteristics; expensive pairs "depreciate more in bad times" when liquidity and sentiment are low; authors account for "all transaction costs and limitations to trade" — **[PR]** [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rasset:v:12:y:2022:i:3:p:667-705.)
- Survey: cross-exchange arbitrage spread on all 510 BTC-fiat pairs 0.685/week (t = 4.46), but on the 226 investable open-market pairs 0.038 (t = 12.45); "the apparent 'free lunch' largely vanishes" — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)

**Low volatility / betting-against-beta / downside beta**
- Burggraf & Rudolf, "Cryptocurrencies and the low volatility anomaly", *Finance Research Letters* 40 (2021): 1,000 coins, 28 Apr 2013 – 1 Nov 2019, long-short vol-sorted portfolios: "no evidence of a significant low volatility premium", robust to sample size, rebalancing and construction — **[PR]** [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics154461232030667x.html)
- Kaya & Mostowfi, "Low-volatility strategies for highly liquid cryptocurrencies", *FRL* 46 (2021/22), art. 102422: liquid coins Jan 2017 – Jun 2021; concentrated low-vol portfolios with 6-12-month look-back/holding earn "statistically significant excess returns"; a simple stop-loss reduces downside and "significantly improves the Sharpe ratios" (hump-shaped in the threshold); costs: not stated in what I read — **[PR]** [ZHAW repository](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/handle/11475/23853?mode=full)
- Leong et al. (lead Simon Kwok), "The pricing of jump and diffusive risks in the cross-section of cryptocurrency returns", *Journal of Empirical Finance* (2023): >4,000 coins, 2015-2022; highest-idiosyncratic-risk coins averaged −9.36 % p.a., lowest +80.6 % p.a.; exploiting it "would need to borrow at least 50 % of their investment" with "potential loss of −84 %"; costs not quantified, wide spreads flagged (university press release, title per phys.org citation) — **[PR via press release]** [U. Sydney](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2023/11/01/huge-crypto-study-finds-high-risk-doesnt-equal-reward.html); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2023-11-crypto-high-doesnt-equal-reward.html)
- An *Annals of Operations Research* (2024) paper reports the opposite: "the additional risk measured by idiosyncratic volatility is well priced in cryptocurrencies" (search summary only) — **[PR, summary]** [Springer mirror](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10479-022-04568-9.pdf)
- Dobrynskaya, "Is Downside Risk Priced in Cryptocurrency Market?" (HSE WP 79/FE/2020): 1,700 coins; higher downside beta → higher average return, priced "with a statistically significant premium"; adding downside risk improves CAPM and 3-factor fit. Search summary adds a long-high / short-low downside-beta spread of ≈2.6 % per week and an insignificant/negative upside-beta premium (not verified in PDF) — **[WP]** [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:hig:wpaper:79/fe/2020); [HSE PDF](https://wp.hse.ru/data/2020/09/16/1583081932/79FE2020.pdf)

**Quality**
- No peer-reviewed asset-pricing paper tests a GitHub/developer-activity "quality" factor. The only numeric evidence is a vendor study of 274 of the top-300 coins over 12 Feb – 14 Mar 2026 reporting a +0.28 correlation of a GitHub score with forward returns (one month, no costs) — **[BL/vendor]** [CryptoScores](https://www.cryptoscores.com/blog/fundamental-signals-cryptocurrency-performance)

### Inferences
- The investable residue of the crypto factor zoo for a BTC/ETH/XAUT options trader on Delta India is thin: the size premium is a micro-cap effect, the value premium is an altcoin on-chain ratio, and neither is tradeable with three underlyings. What transfers is the risk picture: crypto has no low-beta premium and no reward for idiosyncratic volatility in most studies, so leverage-for-yield ("sell vol on high-beta alts") is not supported by a priced factor.
- Where crypto factor results flip between papers (low-vol, downside beta, idiosyncratic vol) the drivers are universe (micro-caps vs liquid coins), weighting and sub-period; treat any single-paper factor claim as unproven until a second team replicates on liquid coins post-2020 with costs.

### Gaps
- Exact weekly returns and t-stats from Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu (2022) and from Cong et al. could not be read (PDF text extraction failed / 403); the survey's 2013-2025 re-estimates are used instead.
- Dobrynskaya's 2.6 %/week figure and frequency come from a search summary, not the paper.
- No study was found that tests a crypto quality/profitability analogue with a return test.

## KQ2. Does volatility targeting improve risk-adjusted returns of a BTC long or trend strategy?

### Takeaway
The traditional-market evidence (Moreira-Muir; Harvey et al.) says vol scaling raises Sharpe for "risk assets" with a leverage effect and cuts left tails everywhere; for crypto there is no peer-reviewed test I could find, but Man AHL (with costs) and Concretum (gross) report that volatility scaling is the core of a workable crypto trend program and that vol-targeted long-only BTC earns a Sharpe just under 0.8 (2018-25). The robust, repeatedly documented benefit is drawdown/tail reduction; the Sharpe gain on a raw BTC long is not established.

### Cited Findings
- Moreira & Muir, "Volatility-Managed Portfolios", *Journal of Finance* 2017: scale monthly factor returns by inverse previous-month realised variance; Sharpe ratios rise "by 50 % to 100 %" for market, value, momentum, profitability, ROE and investment factors (US equities) — **[PR]** [NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208.pdf)
- A replication (SSRN, via Scribd) reports the gain for the US market factor is smaller than claimed: annualised Sharpe 0.5225 managed vs 0.4155 buy-and-hold — **[WP, scraped copy]** [Scribd SSRN-id3832340](https://www.scribd.com/document/515739936/SSRN-id3832340)
- Harvey, Hoyle, Korgaonkar, Rattray, Sargaison & Van Hemert, "The Impact of Volatility Targeting", *Journal of Portfolio Management* 45(1), Fall 2018: >60 assets, daily data from as early as 1926; Sharpe gain "only holds for 'risk assets', such as equity and credit", linked to the leverage effect; for bonds, currencies, commodities the Sharpe effect is negligible; left-tail events "less severe" for all assets because high vol → small notional; also improves 60/40 and risk-parity portfolios. Crypto not covered — **[PR]** [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting-on-equities-bonds-commodities-and-currencies); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/volatility-targeting-improves-risk-adjusted-returns/)
- Man AHL, "In Crypto We Trend" (19 Dec 2024): average pairwise crypto correlation ≈0.6; MA-crossover (e.g. 50/200-day) and breakout trend models; Sharpe calculations include "transaction costs, shorting costs, liquidity constraints and shorting availability"; optimal Sharpe with ~10-15 coins (crossover) or ~10 (breakout); "once volatility is scaled, the left tails of Bitcoin are more benign than those of the S&P 500"; Sharpe values only in figures, not in text — **[PF]** [Man](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend)
- Concretum Group, intraday BTC trend note (c. Jan 2026): 2018-2025; long-short intraday trend ensemble vol-scaled to 20 % earned a gross Sharpe ≈1.6; a vol-targeted (20 %) long-only Bitcoin portfolio ≈0.8 (gross/net not stated) — **[PF]** [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/)
- Quantpedia (6 May 2026), gold/bitcoin dual momentum, 31 Dec 2018 – Apr 2026, weekly rebalance, costs not stated: BTC buy-and-hold 46.66 % p.a., vol 64.01 %, Sharpe 0.73, MaxDD −77.49 %; vol-capped composite (weight = min(1, 20 %/σ₁₂ᵂ)) 12.01 % p.a., vol 8.77 %, Sharpe 1.37, MaxDD −12.27 % vs uncapped composite 64.73 % p.a., Sharpe 1.47, MaxDD −49.40 % → vol cap trades return for drawdown at almost unchanged Sharpe — **[PF]** [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/dual-momentum-allocation-between-physical-gold-and-bitcoin-digital-gold/?a=6080)
- Jones, Matsui & Knottenbelt, "Stablecoins as Dry Powder" (arXiv 2603.23480, 24 Mar 2026): a crypto vol-targeting model "shows lower risk when stablecoin factors are included"; search summary shows a 20 % target with lower return, lower vol and smaller MaxDD than buy-and-hold but a lower Sortino (1.96 vs 2.47); table not readable, costs not stated — **[WP]** [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23480)
- Barbon, "Catching Crypto Trends" (SSRN WP, rev. 9 Apr 2025): Donchian ensemble + volatility-based sizing on the top-20 liquid coins since 2015, survivorship-bias-free; net-of-fees Sharpe >1.5, annualised alpha 10.8 % vs bitcoin; drawdowns not on page — **[WP]** [abarbon.com](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends)
- Medium/Coinmonks (anonymous): vol-managed crypto baskets had ">3X the Sharpe ratio and 1/3 the drawdowns" of equal-weight (from late 2019; no costs) — **[BL]** [Medium](https://medium.com/coinmonks/whats-vol-got-to-do-with-it-886b57c2d762)
- Context on regime: coin-market weekly SD 11.37 % pre-2020 vs 9.00 % post-2020; MaxDD −89.82 % full sample, −77.57 % post-2020; share of 5-minute intervals with a significant jump: BTC 40.0 % pre-2020 → 18.8 % post-2020 (US stocks ≈5.2-5.6 %) — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)

### Inferences
- BTC has a documented leverage-type effect (vol spikes in drawdowns, jumps cluster), which is the mechanism Harvey et al. say makes vol targeting Sharpe-positive; so a Sharpe gain is plausible but unproven for a BTC long — the only direct numbers (Quantpedia, arXiv 2603) show roughly unchanged or slightly lower risk-adjusted return and a large drawdown cut.
- For an options desk, the practical implication is sizing by realised/implied vol rather than by notional, which is what every surviving crypto trend program does.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper tests vol-managed BTC or crypto trend with Sharpe before/after, costs included. Harvey et al. 2018 exclude crypto; the Duke PDF was not readable so the exact Sharpe deltas per asset class are not quoted.

## KQ3. Which on-chain / flow signals have been tested with proper statistics, and what did they find?

### Takeaway
Formal tests exist for stablecoin flows (positive, mostly intraday), ETF flows (contemporaneous, not predictive), exchange BTC flows (weak/inconsistent), network adoption (explains ~8 % of return variation, no post-announcement drift) and funding (predictable itself, not shown to predict spot). MVRV, SOPR, miner reserves and Puell have **no** peer-reviewed predictive test that I could find — they remain chart folklore; the one academic test of sentiment/technical/S2F/Metcalfe measures found no out-of-sample power.

### Cited Findings

**Stablecoin supply / flows**
- Chi, Chu & Hao, "Return and Volatility Forecasting Using On-Chain Flows in Cryptocurrency Markets" (arXiv 2411.06327, v2 Sep 2025): 2017-2023, 1-6 h intervals; USDT net inflow to exchanges positively predicts BTC and ETH returns (strongest at 1 h per abstract v1); BTC net inflows "generally lack predictive power for BTC returns, except at 4 hours", negatively associated with volatility at all intervals; ETH inflows negatively predict ETH returns; no p-values, OOS or costs in abstract — **[WP]** [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327)
- Ante, Fiedler & Strehle, "The influence of stablecoin issuances on cryptocurrency markets", *FRL* 41 (2021): event study; market downturn in the week before issuance; positive abnormal returns in the 24 h before and after; USDT effect in the 24 h before, HUSD/BUSD after; issuance size insignificant. Companion paper: 1,587 stablecoin transfers ≥$1m, Apr 2019 – Mar 2020, show significant abnormal BTC volume and returns around transfers — **[PR]** [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v41y2021ics1544612320316810.html); [BRL WP 11](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/The_Influence_of_Stablecoin_Issuances_on_Cryptocurrency_Markets_BRL_Working_Paper_No_11.pdf)
- Contradiction on Tether: Griffin & Shams argue unbacked issuance moved BTC; Wei, and Lyons & Viswanath-Natraj, find no systematic effect of Tether issuance on BTC/ETH prices — **[PR]** [VoxEU](https://voxeu.org/article/stable-coins-dont-inflate-crypto-markets); [arXiv survey 2507.13883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13883)
- Jones, Matsui & Knottenbelt (2026): copula methods find in-sample causality from stablecoin volume/upside volatility to crypto volatility at daily/weekly/monthly horizons and lower forecasting MSE — **[WP]** [arXiv 2603.23480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23480)

**ETF flows**
- Mazur & Polyzos, "Spot Bitcoin ETFs: The Effect of Fund Flows on Bitcoin Price Formation", *Journal of Alternative Investments* 27(4), 2025, pp. 110-123: first weeks of US spot ETF trading; net flows are "a strong positive predictor of bitcoin price levels" with R² 95 %; most price change occurs outside ETF hours. Note: a level-on-cumulative-flow regression over weeks is not a return-predictability test — **[PR]** [ZU Scholars](https://zuscholars.zu.ac.ae/works/7267)
- DWF Labs (report 22 Oct 2026): 694 trading days from Jan 2024; correlation of daily ETF flows with BTC change over the **prior** 5 sessions 0.54, with the **next** 5 sessions 0.03; outflows in 7 of 8 ≥15 % drops, inflows in all 9 ≥15 % rallies; ≈one-fifth of flows possibly hedged via CME; "ETF flows confirm moves; they don't predict them" — **[PF]** [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-etf-flows-follow-price-trends-dwf-labs)
- NYDIG: weekend funding costs associated with 39 % lower outflows and 28 % lower inflows (mechanical, not predictive) — **[PF, summary]** [NYDIG](https://www.nydig.com/research/predicting-bitcoin-etf-fund-flows)

**Exchange flows, miner flows, MVRV, SOPR**
- Chi et al. above is the only formal exchange-flow test found: BTC exchange net inflow is not a reliable return predictor — **[WP]** [arXiv 2411.06327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327)
- A Granger test on a DEX pool finds causality from BTC returns to liquidity changes, not the reverse — **[WP]** [arXiv 2402.03953](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03953)
- MVRV/SOPR: Coin Metrics describes MVRV ≈3.0 marking 2021 local tops and SOPR as a profit-taking proxy — descriptive, "a handful of observations, not a statistical test" — **[PF]** [Coin Metrics SOTN 97](https://coinmetrics.substack.com/p/coin-metrics-state-of-the-network-094); Santiment notes MVRV-Z >6 near four tops (anecdotal) — **[PF]** [Santiment](https://insights.santiment.net/read/one-upping-your-buy-low-sell-high-game-with-mvrv-z-score-7121)
- No peer-reviewed paper testing MVRV, SOPR, Puell multiple or miner reserves as return predictors surfaced in two targeted searches. Nearest: a Lynn University conditional-regression/logistic strategy on miner revenue (17 trading-day test, anecdotal) — **[WP]** [Lynn](https://scholars.lynn.edu/en/publications/bitcoin-poison-anecdotal-evidence-from-bitcoin-miners-revenue/); VanEck claim that 90-day forward BTC returns were positive 65 % of the time after hash-rate declines vs 54 % after increases (report, no test) — **[PF via media]** [CoinTurk](https://en.coin-turk.com/bitcoin-mining-trends-predict-rise-in-crypto-prices/)

**Network adoption**
- Survey: on-chain new-user growth explains ≈8 % of return variation (>10 % for largest coins); prices react on the weekly adoption release with "muted pre-announcement behavior and no post-announcement drift" — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)

**Funding rate**
- He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter, "Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures" (June 2024 draft): Binance hourly BTC/ETH 8 Jan 2020 – 11 Mar 2024 (36,578 obs); funding is a weighted average of past futures-spot gaps, so "backward-looking by construction"; past 120-day spot returns explain the basis (R² 0.09-0.33); the paper "does not test whether funding predicts future spot returns"; basis-threshold trade Sharpe BTC 3.53 (no costs) / 1.80 (high costs), ETH 4.83 / 2.55; for BTC 8.64 pp of 13.70 % total return is price convergence, 5.06 pp funding; funding's contribution "seems to diminish over time" — **[WP]** [arXiv 2212.06888v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v5)
- Inan, "Predictability of funding rates", *IRFA* 119 (2026), art. 105464: BTC/USDT 8-hour funding 11 Jan 2024 – 17 Mar 2025, last 384 obs out of sample; finds funding is predictable but value "only when they improve decisions after costs" (search summary; paper not read) — **[PR, summary]** [DOI 10.1016/j.irfa.2026.105464](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2026.105464)
- Survey: crypto carry Sharpe 6.45 over 2020-25, falling to 4.06 from 2024 and negative in 2025; mean funding return ≈8 %, vol 0.8 % — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- Vendor heuristics (8-h funding >0.03 % precedes corrections; deep negative = bottom) are untested — **[BL/PF]** [CryptoQuant](https://cryptoquant.com/asset/btc/chart/derivatives/funding-rates); [Sharpe.ai](https://www.sharpe.ai/futures/bitcoin/funding-rate)

**Sentiment / attention / social volume**
- Shelton, "Bitcoin Return Prediction: Is It Possible via Stock-to-Flow, Metcalfe's Law, Technical Analysis, or Market Sentiment?", *JRFM* 17(10):443 (2024): S2F and Metcalfe fit in-sample but have "limited to no ability to predict Bitcoin's returns out-of-sample"; sentiment and technical measures "generally unrelated to Bitcoin's returns in-sample and are poor predictors … out-of-sample" — **[PR]** [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jjrfmx/v17y2024i10p443-d1490211.html)
- Liu & Tsyvinski (NBER 24877, 2018): Google searches for "Bitcoin" forecast next-week returns (1-2 weeks BTC; 1 week XRP; 1-6 weeks ETH) — **[WP]** [CryptoGlobe summary](https://cryptoglobe.com/latest/2018/08/google-search-volume-can-predict-crypto-price-movements-study-suggests); but a 2021 *Financial Innovation* paper finds returns drive attention and attention affects returns "only occasionally … mostly during bubbles", and Urquhart (2018) found no effect — **[PR]** [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/s40854-021-00275-9)
- Twitter: Kraaijeveld & De Smedt (2020) find predictive power for several coins; a LASSO-VAR study finds Granger causality for most coins but **reversed** (returns → sentiment) for Bitcoin; another finds no Granger causality at all — **[WP/PR]** [arXiv 2210.00883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00883)
- Fear & Greed index: only a Corporate Ownership & Control paper (monthly ECM) and a Farsi master's thesis (62 % next-day directional accuracy, index change strongest predictor) — weak evidence, short index history — **[PR-low / thesis]** [Virtus](https://virtusinterpress.org/IMG/pdf/cocv21i2art10.pdf); [Sharif thesis](https://repository.sharif.edu/resource/501394/how-people--39-s-sentiment-and-attention-affect-the-return-of-bitcoin)

### Inferences
- The statistically tested signals point the same way: money-in (stablecoin inflow, adoption growth) is contemporaneously/short-horizon positive; price-derived indicators (MVRV, SOPR, F&G, funding) are functions of past price and add nothing beyond momentum in the one paper that checked (Shelton 2024). For a desk, stablecoin exchange inflow and network adoption are the only on-chain inputs with a formal result, and both are intraday-to-weekly, not cycle-timing tools.
- ETF-flow "signals" are the clearest case of flow following price (0.54 lagged vs 0.03 lead correlation).

### Gaps
- No proper test of MVRV, SOPR, Puell, miner reserves, or the stablecoin supply ratio as return predictors with out-of-sample statistics; Glassnode/CryptoQuant publish charts, not tests.
- Chi et al. significance levels, costs and OOS design not visible; Inan paper read via summary only.

## KQ4. Macro-event strategies: pre-FOMC drift and its crypto analogue; CPI-day BTC; turn-of-the-month

### Takeaway
The equity pre-FOMC drift (≈49 bp per meeting, >80 % of the 1994-2011 equity premium) has faded in equities and has no peer-reviewed crypto analogue — crypto FOMC studies find volatility, not a drift. CPI days: BTC falls ≈5.5 % per 1 pp upside surprise in the first hour (2020-26, R² ≈25 %, strongest 2022-23), confirmed directionally by Pinchuk (−24 bp per 1 s.d.); this is a reaction, not a pre-positionable edge, and no after-cost strategy is documented. Turn-of-the-month in BTC is mixed (one positive risk-adjusted paper, two null results).

- Crypto FOMC: Thai journal (2021-23, 5-min, GARCH) finds declining BTC/ETH volatility around FOMC; Jarboui & Mnif (*J. - Rodriguez & Colombo (MPRA 2024): monthly VAR Aug 2010 – Jan 2023 finds BTC returns **rise** after positive inflation shocks (monthly, context-specific, "likely to fade") — conflicts with the intraday results — **[WP]** [MPRA 120477](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/120477/)
- Volmex: BVIV range rises 104.3 points in the hour after CPI (method/sample not verifiable) — **[PF]** [Volmex PDF](https://volmex.finance/Macroeconomic%20Announcements%20Fuel%20Volatility%20An%20In-Depth%20Analysis.pdf)
- Turn-of-month: Vasileiou, *Int. J. - The tradeable content of CPI/FOMC for an options desk is the vol event, not direction: the documented facts are (i) BTC implied/realised vol spikes into and through the release, (ii) direction is a same-hour reaction to the surprise, which cannot be known in advance. - Pre-FOMC drift is a decayed equity anomaly; the crypto papers that looked found volatility effects, not returns. Treat any "BTC always dumps before FOMC" claim as untested.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper on a 24-hour pre-FOMC return in BTC; no after-cost event strategy for CPI or FOMC in crypto.
- Vasileiou's sample period and separate BTC/ETH statistics are behind a paywall.

## KQ5. Dual momentum / absolute momentum (Antonacci) and the Faber 10-month SMA on BTC

### Takeaway
No published, cost-inclusive backtest of Antonacci's 12-month absolute momentum or Faber's 10-month SMA on BTC exists in the sources found. The nearest credible evidence: weekly dual momentum BTC-vs-gold works best at 4-12-week lookbacks (Sharpe 1.47-1.64 vs 0.73 buy-and-hold, 2018-26, costs not stated); academic BTC moving-average studies find trend rules beat buy-and-hold on Sharpe but with unstable parameters and zero assumed costs; the 2025 survey finds 12- and 24-week cross-sectional momentum insignificant, which cautions against long lookbacks in crypto.

## KQ6. What does the overfitting / post-publication literature say, and what should "proven" mean?

### Takeaway
Published equity predictors lose ≈26 % of their return out of sample and ≈58 % post-publication (McLean-Pontiff), with later work showing ≈93 % decay after costs; Harvey-Liu-Zhu show that after ~316 tested factors a new one needs t > 3.0, not 2.0; Bailey-López de Prado show that ≈45 random configurations on five years of data almost guarantee an in-sample Sharpe of 1 with expected OOS Sharpe 0. In crypto the same pattern is already visible: size weakened post-2020, 12/24-week momentum never worked, carry's Sharpe went from 6.45 to negative in 2025, and exchange arbitrage vanishes on investable pairs. "Proven" should therefore mean: an economic reason, replication by a second team, survival in the post-2020 subsample, costs included, a deflated/multiple-testing-adjusted Sharpe, and a reported trial count.

### Cited Findings
- McLean & Pontiff, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?", *J. Finance* 2016: 97 predictors; returns 26 % lower out-of-sample and 58 % lower post-publication; the 32-point difference attributed to publication-informed trading; the OOS drop is "an upper bound estimate of data mining effects" — **[PR]** [vLex copy](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/does-academic-research-destroy-855658380); [ETF.com summary](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-published-results-impact-future-results)
- Follow-up (*Quantitative Finance* 2022): gross anomaly returns decay ≈50 % post-publication, 72 % using post-2005 data, and 93 % after total trading costs — **[PR]** [T&F](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2022.2098810)
- Jacobs & Müller, "Anomalies across the globe: Once public, no longer existent?", *JFE* 2020: 241 anomalies, 39 markets; the US is the only country with a reliable post-publication decline — **[PR]** [Uni Duisburg-Essen](https://hemf.wiwi.uni-due.de/forschung/publikationen/anomalies-across-the-globe-once-public-no-longer-existent-14591)
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu, "… and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", *RFS* 29(1), 2016, pp. 5-68: 313 published + 63 near-published papers, 316 factors; multiple-testing framework; "a newly discovered factor needs to clear a much higher hurdle, with a t-ratio greater than 3.0" — **[PR]** [NBER w20592](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:20592); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/taming-the-factor-zoo/)
- Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023) counter-view: 82.4 % of anomalies replicate using CAPM alphas — **[PR, summary]** [Systematically Biased](https://systematicallybiased.substack.com/p/pockets-of-replicability-post5)
- Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism" / "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" (*J. Computational Finance*): testing >45 configurations on a 5-year sample "is almost guaranteed to identify configurations with an in-sample annualized Sharpe ratio of one" with expected OOS Sharpe zero; Minimum Backtest Length rises with the number of trials N; recommendations: demand a higher in-sample Sharpe as trials rise, report the number of trials, distrust complex strategies, beware memory/renormalisation, and the Sharpe-based derivations assume "tame" distributions — **[PR]** [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/insidiousness-of-overfitting-investment-strategies-via-iterative-backtests); [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [Carma PDF](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)
- Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio", *J. Portfolio Management* 2014: corrects the Sharpe for selection bias from multiple testing and for non-normal (skewed, fat-tailed) returns; keywords include Minimum Track Record Length and Minimum Backtest Length (abstract only) — **[PR]** [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); PBO framework uses combinatorially symmetric cross-validation — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)
- Crypto decay examples (all from the 2025 survey, 2013-Sep 2025): size t −2.66 → −1.69 post-2020; carry Sharpe 6.45 (2020-25) → 4.06 from 2024 → negative in 2025; cross-exchange arb 0.685/week on all pairs → 0.038 on investable pairs — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4); Fieberg et al. 2024: anomaly returns "fade over time" and sit in bull markets — **[PR]** [ICM](https://open.icm.edu.pl/items/2350d455-72e4-4845-a442-9c4024be7fb6/full); Rozario et al.: Sharpe maxima "non-robust and unstable" — **[WP]** [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.12155)
- Shelton 2024: S2F/Metcalfe fit in-sample, fail out-of-sample — the crypto template for a fitted narrative — **[PR]** [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jjrfmx/v17y2024i10p443-d1490211.html)
- Mercik et al.: specific crypto factors show "limited temporal persistence" — **[WP]** [ICM](https://open.icm.edu.pl/items/d9f44e22-0614-4db8-8585-6dcba8906bb5/full)

### Inferences
- Checklist a trader should demand before calling a strategy "proven" (derived from the above): (1) t-stat > 3 or deflated Sharpe > 0 given the number of trials actually run; (2) the trial count disclosed; (3) a held-out period that was never touched, ideally the post-2020 regime for crypto; (4) a parameter plateau (neighbouring lookbacks also work), not a single peak; (5) costs at the venue's real fee schedule (Delta: 0.01 % option fee, 3.5 % premium cap, GST per GAPS #129); (6) at least one independent replication; (7) an economic mechanism that survives publication (risk compensation or structural constraint, not a pattern).
- Crypto's short history (≈12 years of liquid data, ≈6 post-2020) means the Minimum Backtest Length binds hard: with monthly data there are only ~70 post-2020 observations, so monthly rules (Faber 10-month, 12-month absolute momentum) cannot be statistically "proven" on BTC at all; weekly/daily rules can, but only with few trials.

### Gaps
- DSR and MinBTL closed-form formulas not retrieved (SSRN 403; PDFs unreadable); only the 45-configurations/5-years benchmark and qualitative recommendations are cited.
- No crypto-specific paper applies the deflated Sharpe ratio or PBO to published crypto anomalies.

## KQ7. Which position-sizing rules are evidence-based for a fat-tailed asset like BTC?

### Takeaway
The theory (Kelly; MacLean-Thorp-Ziemba) is settled: full Kelly maximises long-run growth but "it is possible, through bad scenarios, to lose most of one's wealth", and over-betting raises ruin risk; fractional Kelly (commonly ¼-½) is the standard response to estimation error and fat tails. Crypto-specific evidence is thin: vol-scaling to a 20 % target cuts BTC drawdowns from −77 % to −12 % (Quantpedia), simple stop-loss rules raise Sharpe in low-vol crypto portfolios (Kaya-Mostowfi), and Black-Litterman on post-2020 moments puts BTC at 3.1 % (retail) to 5.5 % (institutional) of a portfolio. "1-2 % risk per trade" and "half-Kelly" are practitioner conventions without a crypto test.

### Cited Findings
- MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba, "Long-term capital growth: the good and bad properties of the Kelly and fractional Kelly capital growth criteria", *Quantitative Finance* 2010 (and 2011 World Scientific chapter): Kelly "maximizes the limiting exponential growth rate of wealth"; wagers can be very large because Arrow-Pratt risk aversion is small, so it is "relatively risky in the short term"; "it is possible, through bad scenarios, to lose most of one's wealth"; fractional Kelly reduces risk "at the cost of lower expected final wealth"; "care in the use of Kelly and fractional Kelly strategies is crucial" — **[PR]** [EconPapers chapter](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wsi:wschap:9789811285530_0015); [Berkeley PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)
- Wikipedia synthesis with sources: betting more than Kelly increases ruin risk (MacLean-Thorp-Ziemba 2010); Kelly is "perfectly valid only for fully known outcome probabilities"; overestimating edge raises ruin risk; significant downside tail risk is a reason to cut to half-Kelly (Turlakov 2017); Thorp (2008, *Wilmott*) gives reducing volatility and protecting against edge-estimation error as the practical reasons for fractions; no closed-form numbers for half-Kelly growth/variance on the page — **[tertiary]** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion)
- Practitioner convention: professionals typically use 25-75 % of computed Kelly; fractional Kelly as margin of safety for estimation error; crypto correlation between positions violates the independent-bets assumption; 0.1-0.25× Kelly suggested for illiquid tokens — **[BL]** [Altrady](https://www.altrady.com/blog/risk-management/kelly-criterion-crypto-position-sizing); [tastylive Monte Carlo on iron condors: bigger size → better mean, higher ruin; "stay small"](https://www.tastylive.com/shows/market-measures/episodes/kelly-criterion-the-secret-to-trading-longevity-05-06-2025)
- Vol targeting as sizing: Quantpedia composite dual momentum with weight = min(100 %, 20 %/σ) cut MaxDD from −49.40 % to −12.27 % and vol from 44.14 % to 8.77 % at Sharpe 1.37 vs 1.47 (2018-26, costs not stated) — **[PF]** [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/dual-momentum-allocation-between-physical-gold-and-bitcoin-digital-gold/?a=6080); Harvey et al. 2018: vol targeting makes left-tail events "less severe" across all asset classes — **[PR]** [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf); Man AHL: vol-scaled BTC left tails "more benign than those of the S&P 500" — **[PF]** [Man](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend)
- Stop-loss evidence: Kaya & Mostowfi (FRL 2021/22) — a simple stop-loss "reduces the downside risk … and significantly improves the Sharpe ratios", hump-shaped in the threshold; cites Białkowski (2020) that industry stop-loss rules roughly halve crypto volatility and returns — **[PR]** [ZHAW](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/handle/11475/23853?mode=full)
- Fat-tail facts to size against: coin-market MaxDD −89.82 % (2013-25), −77.57 % post-2020; weekly SD 9-11 %; BTC jump share 18.8 % of 5-min intervals post-2020 vs ≈5 % for US stocks; Black-Litterman allocation ≈3.1 % retail / 5.5 % institutional, "illustrative upper bounds" — **[WP]** [arXiv 2510.14435v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- Leong/Kwok et al.: exploiting the low-risk anomaly needs ≥50 % borrowing with a potential −84 % loss — a worked example of why leverage on a thin crypto edge is ruinous — **[PR via press]** [U. Sydney](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2023/11/01/huge-crypto-study-finds-high-risk-doesnt-equal-reward.html)
- Bailey et al. caution that Sharpe-based inference assumes tame distributions; the Deflated Sharpe explicitly corrects for skew and kurtosis — relevant because Kelly inputs (edge, variance) are estimated from the same fat-tailed data — **[PR]** [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)

### Inferences
- For BTC/ETH options on Delta India the evidence-consistent stack is: defined-risk structures (removes the ruin tail Kelly cannot handle), size by realised/implied vol to a fixed risk budget (the only rule with crypto numbers), cap total exposure well below full Kelly (half or less, because edge estimates from ≤6 years of regime data are noisy), and use a strategy-level stop (Kaya-Mostowfi evidence) rather than leg-level stops that fight gamma. "1-2 % per trade" is a heuristic consistent with these but not itself tested.
- The −77 % post-2020 drawdown and 19 % jump frequency imply that any sizing rule calibrated on daily vol will understate gap risk; options with defined max loss are the structural answer, which is why the literature's vol-scaling results should be read as upper bounds for a linear position.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of fractional Kelly or percent-risk-per-trade on BTC; no crypto paper quantifying half-Kelly growth/variance trade-off; the MacLean-Thorp-Ziemba full text (with the half-Kelly growth ≈ ¾ / variance ≈ ½ style results) was not readable, so those numbers are **not** quoted here.
- Kaya-Mostowfi stop-loss thresholds and Sharpe deltas are in the PDF, not the abstract.
