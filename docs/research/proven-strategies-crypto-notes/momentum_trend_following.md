Research date: 8 Nov 2026. Confidence tags used throughout: **[PR]** peer-reviewed journal; **[WP]** academic working paper / preprint (not refereed); **[FIRM]** practitioner-firm research or press coverage of it; **[BLOG]** blog, vendor or anonymous backtest (lowest confidence). Where I only saw an abstract or a secondary summary, I say so. Pre-2020 sources are flagged "(pre-2020)" and I note whether later work confirmed them.

## Key Question 1: Is time-series momentum / trend following "proven" in traditional markets, and what happened to CTAs in the last decade?

### Takeaway
Time-series momentum (TSMOM) is one of the best-documented return patterns in finance: positive in every one of 58 futures contracts over 1985-2009 (Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen) and positive net of 2/20 fees in every decade since 1880 (Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen, full-sample net Sharpe 0.77). The weak spot is 2010-2018, when the SG Trend Index Sharpe collapsed to roughly 0.05, and 2023-2025, when live trend indices were flat to negative; the strategy is "proven" as a long-run premium with multi-year flat spells, not as a steady earner.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] (pre-2020, confirmed by later work)** Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, "Time Series Momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 104(2), 2012, pp. 228-250: the signal is the sign of each instrument's trailing 12-month excess return with a one-month holding period, over 58 liquid futures (equity indices, currencies, commodities, developed government bonds); "12-month time series momentum profits are positive, not just on average across these assets, but for every asset contract we examine (58 in total)". AQR publishes the factor data for Jan 1985-Dec 2009. — [EconPapers listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:104:y:2012:i:2:p:228-250); [AQR dataset](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463). I did not see the paper's own Sharpe table; secondary summaries disagree (Quantpedia lists 1.31, a blog says about 1.0 for the diversified portfolio), so treat the exact figure as unverified. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/time-series-momentum-effect/); [Menon Lab blog](https://themenonlab.blog/blog/time-series-momentum-hedge-fund-strategy-explained)
- **[FIRM] (AQR, 2014 version; later published in JPM 2017)** Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing": equal-weighted combination of 1-month, 3-month and 12-month time-series momentum across 67 markets in four asset classes, each position scaled to equal ex-ante volatility, portfolio volatility target 10%, Jan 1880-Dec 2013; costs simulated from current transaction-cost estimates (2x higher 1993-2002, 6x higher 1880-1992) plus a 2% management / 20% performance fee with high-water mark. Full-sample results: 14.9% gross, 11.2% net of 2/20, 9.7% realised volatility, **net Sharpe 0.77**, correlation -0.02 to the S&P 500 and -0.04 to US 10-year bonds. Net-of-fee return was positive in every decade; by-decade net Sharpe ranged from 0.13 (1900s) to 1.70 (1960s); 1990s 0.62; **Jan 2000-Dec 2013: 11.3% gross, 7.9% net** (Sharpe not legible in my extraction). Ten largest net drawdowns were -26.3% (worst), -25.3%, -23.9%, -21.4%, -17.1%, -15.2%, -14.9%, -14.7%, -14.6%; peak-to-recovery lengths up to 75 months. The authors themselves model a conservative future net Sharpe of 0.4 ("half as large as what we have observed historically") and show a 20% allocation would still have lifted a 60/40 Sharpe from 0.38 to 0.46. — [AQR PDF (oxfordstrat mirror)](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/03/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing.pdf) (text extracted locally with pdftotext). A CXO summary of the 2017 JPM update (1880-2016, 67 markets) quotes a 0.4 average Sharpe after 2/20 fees; a second CXO piece calls 0.4 gross, so the JPM figure is ambiguous in secondary sources. — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/trend-following-with-intrinsic-momentum-over-the-very-long-run)
- **[FIRM]** Mechanism claim in the same paper: trend following did well in bear markets because "most bear markets have historically occurred gradually over several months"; average length of the 10 largest 60/40 drawdowns 1880-2014 was about 15 months. — [AQR PDF](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/03/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing.pdf)
- **[FIRM]** The weak decade: one analysis found the SG Trend Index Sharpe fell to **0.05 over 2010-2018 versus 0.28 since 2000**, attributed to smaller average price moves across 67 markets. — [CME Group](https://cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/the-market-environment-for-trend-following.html) (figure quoted in a search summary; I did not open the full article). 2019: SG Trend Index +9.22%, "best year since 2014". — [Opalesque](https://www.opalesque.com/677098/CTAs_close_best_year_since_2014709.html)
- **[FIRM]** 2022 was the modern high point: SG CTA Index +20.1% for 2022, its best year since inception. — [The Full FX](https://thefullfx.com/?p=3378). 2023: SG Trend Index **-4.16%** for the year. — [Top Traders Unplugged, Dec 2023 report](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/?p=14211)
- **[FIRM]** 2024-2025: BarclayHedge CTA Index +3.45% (2024) and +3.10% (2025); Man AHL Diversified +1.78% (2024), +4.44% (2025). — [Angel Investors Network summary](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/market-analysis/managed-futures-cta-liquid-alternatives-accredited-investor-guide-2026) (secondary). Man AHL Evolution was down 8.5% in 2025 through April after a "miserable 2024". — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/international-trade/quants-chasing-trends-in-exotic-markets-hit-by-commodity-turmoil). A 2025 industry report called CTAs the worst-performing primary hedge-fund strategy after their strong 2022. — [With Intelligence](https://www.withintelligence.com/insights/cta-hedge-fund-report/)
- **[FIRM]** Dao, Nguyen, Deremble, Lemperiere, Bouchaud, Potters (CFM, 2016), "Tail Protection for Long Investors: Trend Convexity at Work": a single-asset trend model on an equal-weighted pool of liquid assets with a roughly 6-month filter and realistic fees reached >80% correlation with the SG CTA Index and matched the industry Sharpe; "long-volatility portfolios lost money consistently over the past two decades, while trend-following posted positive performance". — [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/tail-protection-of-trend-following-strategies/); [CFM](https://www.cfm.com/tail-protection-for-long-investors-convexity-at-work/)

### Inferences
- The "proven" claim rests on breadth (every contract, every decade) rather than on a high Sharpe: 0.4-0.8 net is the realistic planning range, and drawdowns of 20-26% with 3-6 year recoveries are within the historical record even for a 10%-vol diversified book.
- A single-asset implementation (one coin) forfeits most of the diversification that produces the 0.77; AQR's own by-decade range (0.13 to 1.70) shows how wide single-period outcomes are.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen Sharpe table or a year-by-year SG Trend Index table for 2010-2018; both PDFs/pages were paywalled or returned 403. The 0.05 Sharpe figure for 2010-2018 is from a search summary of a CME article.

## Key Question 2: Does cross-sectional momentum hold up in traditional markets (Jegadeesh-Titman; Asness-Moskowitz-Pedersen)?

### Takeaway
Cross-sectional (relative-strength) momentum earned about 1% per month in US stocks 1965-1989 and about 10-11% a year in post-publication out-of-sample periods in the US and internationally; across eight asset classes a 50/50 value-momentum combination had a Sharpe of 1.45. Momentum has survived post-publication, which matters because McLean-Pontiff show the average anomaly loses 58% of its return after publication.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] (pre-2020, confirmed out of sample)** Jegadeesh and Titman, Journal of Finance 48(1), 1993, pp. 65-91: US stocks, 1965-1989, 16 J/K strategies. A Wharton presentation by Jegadeesh shows winners-minus-losers monthly returns after formation (6-month ranking) of 0.91%, 1.22%, 1.03%, 1.07%, 1.01%, 0.87%, 0.72% in months 1-7 (t-stats 2.48 to 3.84), fading to 0.27%, 0.28%, -0.04% in months 8-10 and negative in months 11-12 (-0.19%, -0.48%). The same slides report post-publication performance (Goyal, Jegadeesh, Subrahmanyam 2022): US 1990-2021, 6-month ranking, **10.87% annualised** (t = 2.48); all ex-US 10.67% (t = 3.02); developed ex-US 10.22% (t = 2.57); emerging 9.02% (t = 2.70), samples excluding microcaps and low-priced stocks. — [Jegadeesh slides, Wharton Jacobs Levy Center](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Jegadeesh.pdf) (text extracted locally). Secondary summaries put the original 6/6 strategy at "about 1% per month" / "approximately 12% annualized". — [mql5 mirror of a momentum review](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/218/Momentum.pdf); [StockAlarm blog](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/momentum-investing-strategy)
- **[PR] (pre-2020)** Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen, "Value and Momentum Everywhere", Journal of Finance 2013: sample January 1972 to July 2011; momentum defined for all asset classes as the past 12-month return skipping the most recent month (MOM2-12), and the authors note non-equity momentum is "in fact stronger when we don't skip the most recent month"; value and momentum are negatively correlated within every asset class (secondary summary: average about -0.49); "The 50/50 value and momentum combination portfolio produces an annual Sharpe ratio of 1.45". — [Pedersen's paper PDF](https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf) (text extracted locally; Table I per-asset-class Sharpe columns were not legible in my extraction); [CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/11/value-and-momentum-everywhere-digest-summary)
- **[PR]** McLean and Pontiff, "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?", Journal of Finance 2016: across 97 published predictors, portfolio returns are **26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication**; the decline is larger for predictors with higher in-sample returns. A later study reports about 50% decay, rising to 72% for post-2005 data; a cross-country study of 241 anomalies finds the US is the only country with a reliable post-publication decline. — [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/effects-of-market-adaptation); [JFE 2020 "Anomalies across the globe"](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:1:p:213-230); [Quantitative Finance 2022](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2022.2098810)

### Inferences
- Equity momentum decays within 12 months and reverses after; that is the template against which crypto's much faster (2-4 week) cycle, documented below, should be read.
- Crypto momentum papers were mostly published 2018-2022; by the McLean-Pontiff benchmark a 25-60% haircut on the published premia is the prudent prior.

### Gaps
- Per-asset-class momentum Sharpe ratios from AMP Table I (equities, currencies, commodities, bonds) could not be read from my PDF extraction; not reported.

## Key Question 3: What Sharpe ratios, returns, drawdowns and hit rates are reported for TSMOM / moving-average trend following on BTC and ETH?

### Takeaway
Every academic or firm study I found reports that simple long/flat (or long/short) trend rules on Bitcoin cut maximum drawdown roughly in half (from about -70% to -90% to about -15% to -70% depending on rule and period) and raise the Sharpe ratio versus buy-and-hold, with reported gross Sharpes of 1.7-2.6 on 2010-2023 samples and net Sharpes of 1.25-1.6 once 0.1-0.5% frictions are charged; the price is lower absolute return than holding through bull runs. No peer-reviewed study reports ETH-specific Sharpe figures in text I could access; the Monash and Man AHL work covers ETH but only the direction of the result is visible.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR]** Detzel, Liu, Strauss, Zhou and Zhu, "Learning and Predictability via Technical Analysis: Evidence from Bitcoin and Stocks with Hard-to-Value Fundamentals", Financial Management 50(1), 2021, pp. 107-137 (working paper 2018): price-to-moving-average ratios forecast daily Bitcoin returns in and out of sample; trading strategies on them "produce economically meaningful alpha and Sharpe ratio gains over buy-and-hold". — [WashU profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/learning-and-predictability-via-technical-analysis-evidence-from-/); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:finmgt:v:50:y:2021:i:1:p:107-137). The 2018 working-paper numbers (CXO summary): daily BTC 18 Jul 2010 to 12 Dec 2017, rule = long BTC when price is above its SMA, otherwise T-bills; SMA lengths 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 days. **Buy-and-hold: 224% annualised, 111% vol, Sharpe 2.0, max drawdown -89.5%. SMA rules: 203-216% annualised, 83.8-97.0% vol, Sharpe 2.6 (5-day) down to 2.2 (100-day), max drawdown -66.4% (5-day) to -70.3% (50- and 100-day). Breakeven one-way switching cost 1.1% (5-day) to 4.5% (100-day). Results are gross of costs.** — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/currency-trading/timing-bitcoin-with-smas/)
- **[WP] (abstract + CXO summary only; findings section paywalled)** Le and Ruthbah (Monash Centre for Financial Studies), "Trend-following Strategies for Crypto Investors", SSRN 4551518, Aug 2024: S&P BTC Index from Jan 2016, S&P ETH Index from Jan 2017, S&P Cryptocurrency LargeCap-Ex-MegaCap from Jan 2019, all to Jan 2023; rule = hold the asset when price is above its prior-day SMA, else cash; SMA lengths 20, 65, 150, 200 days; frictions 0.1%, 0.25%, 0.5% per trade. **BTC 20-day SMA: gross Sharpe 1.68, net Sharpe 1.25 at 0.5% frictions.** Monash's project page: shorter lookbacks gave the highest Sharpe but "transaction costs may decrease profits due to frequent portfolio rebalancing"; trend rules "broadly outperformed a constant investment in Bitcoin and Ether". — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/applying-simple-trend-following-rules-to-cryptocurrencies); [Monash project page](https://www.monash.edu/business/mcfs/our-research/all-projects/investment-strategy/trend-following-strategies-for-crypto-investors) (returned 403 on direct fetch; figures from the search summary)
- **[FIRM] (press coverage only; original Man paper not located)** Man AHL researchers found a proxy long-short trend strategy on Bitcoin and on Ether beat buy-and-hold in both coins since 2017 when scaled to 10% volatility; Tarek Abou Zeid (Man AHL) said momentum-type strategies have historically delivered better risk-adjusted returns than buy and hold; Andre Rzym warned against applying momentum models to stablecoins. — [Binance Square repost of Bloomberg, Apr 2024](https://www.binance.com/en-AU/square/post/2024-04-03-quantitative-hedge-fund-investors-find-profit-in-bitcoin-s-volatility-6279507805649); [CryptoGlobe](https://cryptoglobe.com/latest/2024/04/forget-hodling-quantitative-hedge-funds-use-crypto-volatility-to-further-gains). A Thai-language report puts the trend returns at roughly 200-260% vs about 163% buy-and-hold since late 2016; unverified. — [The Standard](https://thestandard.co/?p=919144)
- **[WP]** Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon, "Catching Crypto Trends: A Tactical Approach for Bitcoin and Altcoins", Swiss Finance Institute Research Paper 25-80 / SSRN 5209907, revised 9 Apr 2025: ensemble of Donchian-channel trend models with different lookbacks combined into one signal, volatility-based position sizing, survivorship-bias-free dataset since 2015; **rotational portfolio of the 20 most liquid coins: Sharpe above 1.5 net of fees, annualised alpha 10.8% vs Bitcoin**; the paper also proposes a portfolio-construction technique to reduce trading costs. The abstract pages do not give Bitcoin-only Sharpe, drawdown, lookback lengths or cost assumptions. — [abarbon.com](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2580.html); [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/catching-crypto-trends-a-tactical-approach-for-bitcoin-and-altcoins/)
- **[FIRM/BLOG] Quantpedia**, "Revisiting Trend-following and Mean-reversion Strategies in Bitcoin": daily BTC open prices (Gemini via CryptoDataDownload), 9 Oct 2015 to 20 Aug 2024; **MAX rule = buy on day t when BTC closes at its highest price over the prior x days** (x = 10, 20, 30, 40, 50) with a short forward holding window; original study Nov 2015-Feb 2022, out-of-sample 4 Feb 2022-20 Aug 2024. Conclusion: MAX "stays effective" out-of-sample (10-day lagged 20-day OOS, all lookbacks worthwhile), MIN (buy at x-day low) weakened to low/negative returns; MAX delivered "the bulk of the BTC performance without experiencing severe drawdowns". Sharpe/return/drawdown tables are images only; **no transaction costs, slippage or position sizing are modelled.** — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)
- **[WP]** Rozario, Holt, West and Ng, "A Decade of Evidence of Trend Following Investing in Cryptocurrencies", arXiv 2009.12155, Sep 2020: Bitstamp BTCUSD spot, 13 Sep 2011 to 12 Dec 2019, hourly bars; SMA/EMA/DEMA crossovers with windows optimised on 1-year slices and tested on the following year; "We assumed negligible transaction fees, bid-offer spread, slippage"; headline "255% walkforward annualised returns"; **best full-period in-sample Sharpe: SMA 141h/781h = 1.09, EMA 721h/951h = 1.35, DEMA 791h/981h = 1.32**; but "we find no predictable and attractive Sharpe ratios from using trend following strategies on 1 year training data commencing year Y, for test data commencing year Y+1". — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12155); PDF text extracted locally from [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12155v1.pdf)
- **[WP, unreviewed, internal inconsistencies]** Bui and Nguyen (Talyxion Research), "Systematic Trend-Following with Adaptive Portfolio Construction", arXiv 2602.11708, 2026: Binance perpetuals, 6-hour bars, 150+ pairs, calibration Jan-Dec 2021, out-of-sample Jan 2022-Dec 2024; costs = 4 bp taker fee + linear slippage + 8-hourly funding. Reported OOS net: **AdaptiveTrend 70/30 long/short: 40.5% p.a., 16.8% vol, Sharpe 2.41, MDD -12.7%; Vol-scaled TSMOM (10% target): 22.8%, Sharpe 1.83, MDD -16.1%; TSMOM-1M: 18.4%, Sharpe 0.65, MDD -34.8%; TSMOM-3M: 15.1%, Sharpe 0.54, MDD -38.2%; BTC buy-and-hold: 12.6%, 48.7% vol, Sharpe 0.17, MDD -64.1%; equal-weight top-20 buy-and-hold: 8.3%, Sharpe 0.07, MDD -72.4%.** Win rate by regime: 54.2% bull, 47.8% sideways, 41.3% bear; bear-regime annualised return -4.2%, Sharpe -0.31. Turnover 134%/yr, about 142 trades per month. Sharpe falls from 2.87 to 1.62 as fee sensitivity goes 0 to 12 bp. Caveats: the stated return/vol/rf do not reproduce the 2.41 Sharpe ((40.5-4.5)/16.8 = 2.14), one figure caption cites a different end date, and the paper has one author team and no review. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)
- **[FIRM, vendor]** XBTO "Trend" (long/short trend, universe and signal not disclosed), Jan 2020-Sep 2025: **34.8% annualised vs 61.7% passive BTC; vol 21.5% vs 65.2%; max drawdown -15.5% vs -73%; Sharpe 1.62 vs about 0.95; Sortino 3.83 vs 1.93; Calmar 2.25 vs 0.84; win ratio 63.8% vs 59.4%.** Not stated whether live or simulated, gross or net. — [XBTO](https://www.xbto.com/resources/the-quality-of-returns-crypto-risk-adjusted-performance)
- **[PR] (pre-2020; abstract/search summary only)** Hudson and Urquhart, "Technical trading and cryptocurrencies", Annals of Operations Research 297, 2021, pp. 191-220: 14,919 rules in five families across two Bitcoin markets and three other coins; significant predictability and profitability in-sample for each class in each coin; "breakeven transaction costs ... well above the levels typically seen in cryptocurrency markets"; **out of sample "there is no predictability for Bitcoin", while the other coins retain some.** — [Reading repository](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/85715); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1) (both blocked direct fetch; summary from search results)
- **[BLOG]** 50/200-day crossover, always fully in or out of BTC, 0.1% commission: equity peaked near $644K (from an unstated start) then gave back about 75% of gains; **max drawdown -82.4% lasting 1,662 days**; trend direction changed only 10 times in six years, trades averaged about 6 months; did well in the slow 2022 decline, struggled to stay long in the fast 2021 and 2023 rallies. — [crypto-threads blog](https://crypto-threads-five.vercel.app/post/bitcoin-death-cross-a-data-driven-performance-study)
- **[BLOG]** Daily EMA20 entry with SMA50 filter on 11 years of BTC: Sharpe 1.18 vs 0.86 buy-and-hold, max drawdown 50.9% vs 83.4%. — [personal blog](https://mohit-sharma-website.pages.dev/blogs/btc-ema20-sma50-strategy)

### Inferences
- The consistent, replicable result across confidence tiers is drawdown compression (roughly halving max drawdown) with Sharpe improvement of about 0.3-0.7 over buy-and-hold on the 2015-2024 window; absolute CAGR is usually below buy-and-hold in samples that include 2016-17 and 2020-21.
- Only one academic source (Monash) states a net Sharpe for BTC trend (1.25 at 0.5% friction, 20-day SMA); the 2.2-2.6 figures in Detzel et al. are gross and from 2010-2017 when BTC compounded at 224% a year, so they will not recur.
- Claims above Sharpe 2 net (Talyxion 2.41) come from unreviewed preprints with arithmetic inconsistencies; treat as upper bounds, not expectations.

### Gaps
- No ETH-specific Sharpe, return or drawdown figure from a peer-reviewed or firm source was readable; Monash and Man AHL report ETH only directionally. The Monash SSRN paper's results tables are paywalled.
- Hit rates: only XBTO (63.8%, vendor) and Talyxion (41-54% by regime, preprint) report them; no peer-reviewed hit rate found.
- Zarattini et al. Bitcoin-only results, lookbacks and cost assumptions are in the SSRN full text, which I could not access.

## Key Question 4: Does cross-sectional momentum work across altcoins after costs, and at which horizons?

### Takeaway
Crypto cross-sectional momentum is a 1-4 week phenomenon (Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu: about 3% per week long-short, 4.1% for the 3-week sort, 2014-2018, gross), concentrated in larger coins, and it reverses rather than persists at 1-3 month holding periods (Dobrynskaya); 8-, 16-, 50- and 100-week sorts are not significant. Studies that charge realistic fees, slippage and liquidation risk (Han-Kang-Ryu; Grobys-Sapkota on 143 coins) find weak or no net profit, so the published premium is largely a gross, small-coin, weekly-turnover effect.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR]** Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu, "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency", Journal of Finance 77(2), 2022, pp. 1133-1177 (NBER w25882, 2019): CoinMarketCap coins with market cap above $1 million, 2014-2018, universe growing from 109 coins (2014) to 1,583 (2018); weekly quintile sorts, rebalanced weekly. "We find that the one-, two-, three-, and four-week momentum factors generate statistically significant long-short strategy returns. The results of the zero-investment long-short strategies for the eight-, sixteen-, fifty, and one hundred-week momentum are not statistically significant." Long-short (Q5-Q1) excess returns **2.7% per week (1-week), 3.3% (2-week), 4.1% (3-week, t = 2.74), 2.5% (4-week)**, significant at 5% (1% for 3-week). Size interaction: in the below-median-size group the 3-week momentum long-short earns 0.6% per week (insignificant); in the above-median group **4.2% per week, significant**; "the momentum strategy works better for the larger coins ... in sharp contrast to the equity market". When shorting Bitcoin instead of the loser quintile, the 1-, 2- and 4-week momentum strategies are no longer significant. The paper does not model transaction costs (no "transaction cost" string in the text). — [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882.pdf) (text extracted locally); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3379131); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2022/06/factors-investing-in-cryptocurrency) (403 on fetch)
- **[PR]** Liu and Tsyvinski, "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency", Review of Financial Studies 34(6), 2021, pp. 2689-2727 (NBER w24877, 2018): time-series momentum significant at daily and weekly frequencies for Bitcoin, Ripple and Ethereum, weaker for Ethereum; "a one-standard-deviation increase in the current week's return is associated with a 3.16% increase in returns over a one-week horizon" for Bitcoin. — [VoxEU](https://voxeu.org/article/risks-and-returns-cryptocurrencies) (403 on fetch; figure from the search summary); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:34:y:2021:i:6:p:2689-2727.)
- **[PR] (pre-2020)** Grobys and Sapkota, "Cryptocurrencies and momentum", Economics Letters 180, 2019, pp. 6-10: 143 cryptocurrencies, 2014-2018; "Contrary to earlier studies our findings do not indicate any evidence of significant momentum payoffs"; one-, one-to-six- and one-to-twelve-month cross-sectional strategies not profitable, time-series strategies only marginally so. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ecolet:v:180:y:2019:i:c:p:6-10). A related Grobys and Huhta-Halkola (2019) paper found only weak cross-sectional momentum once small, illiquid coins were excluded. — [Vaasa repository](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/items/99fceb0a-aaaa-4df2-aed1-09e6232a9df3/full)
- **[PR] (pre-2020)** Tzouvanas, Kizys and Tsend-Ayush, "Momentum trading in cryptocurrencies: Short-term returns and diversification benefits", Economics Letters 191, 2020: J/K momentum portfolios on daily data for the 12 largest cryptocurrencies, **transaction cost assumed 26 basis points per trade**; a later paper summarises it as showing momentum "only profitable in the short term for the largest twelve cryptocurrencies"; momentum-portfolio correlations with traditional assets are low. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v191y2020ics0165176519303647.html); [Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/434719/) (PDF fetch 404)
- **[WP]** Dobrynskaya (HSE), cryptocurrency momentum and reversal: about 2,000 coins with cap above $1 million, 2014-2020; sort on past 1-week to 2-year returns, long top 30% / short bottom 30%, hold 1 week to 3 months. **Momentum strongest at 2-week sort / 2-week hold (about +70% annualised); reversal appears from 4-6 week holding periods; a 1-2 week sort with 10-12 week hold loses about -1,000% annualised (long-short, as reported by the university news piece).** The author notes weekly rebalancing "carries significant transaction costs". — [HSE news](https://spb.hse.ru/en/news/559533243.html)
- **[PR, abstract only]** Han, Kang and Ryu (Sungkyunkwan), Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting 2024 (DOI 10.1007/s11156-024-01260-w): estimates costs from actual exchange fees, tick sizes and slippage, and models margin-mode liquidation; "once transaction costs and daily price swings are counted, many momentum portfolios end up liquidated, and many strategies that look significant on paper produce statistically insignificant profits"; strong time-series momentum, weak cross-sectional momentum. — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11156-024-01260-w.pdf) (paywalled; abstract via search)
- **[WP] (pre-2020)** Begusic and Kostanjcar, "Momentum and liquidity in cryptocurrencies", arXiv 1904.00890, 2019: bivariate momentum x Amihud-illiquidity sorts, equal-weighted, rebalanced every two weeks, universe up to about 500 coins 2016-2019; "strong momentum effect in the most liquid cryptocurrencies"; illiquid coins show mean reversion (illiquid losers 1.07% vs illiquid winners 0.53% per period); long-only liquid-winners portfolio beat the cap-weighted market "even when transaction costs are included", but the illiquid-losers portfolio "may additionally deteriorate in practice" from indirect costs. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.00890) (text extracted locally)
- **[WP]** Gbadebo (2026) comparison: time-series momentum 31.96% annual return and better risk-adjusted performance than cross-sectional momentum; cross-sectional showed a 55.0% max drawdown and lower profitability, partly because altcoins are highly correlated. — [AUT working paper PDF](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf) (403 on fetch; figures from search summary)
- **[FIRM]** 2025 practitioner evidence: altcoin-focused quant models suffered "total wipeouts" in the 10 Oct 2025 crash as dozens of tokens fell 40%+ within hours; M-Squared "entirely exited those overly dependent on altcoin order-book depth". — [InvestmentNews, citing Crypto Insights Group](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/crypto-chaos-jolts-hedge-funds-in-worst-year-since-2022-crash/263643)

### Inferences
- Horizon answer: 1-4 weeks yes (gross), 12 months no. The crypto momentum cycle is roughly 12x faster than equities (reversal at about one month versus about one year).
- Cost answer: the gross 3-4% weekly spread needs 100% weekly turnover on both legs, including shorts in coins that may have no borrow or perpetual market; with 26 bp per trade (Tzouvanas) the drag is about 1%/week before slippage and funding, so roughly a third of the gross spread is consumed even at that favourable cost, and more on small coins.
- The "larger coins" result (LTW 4.2%/week above-median size) is the only part of the cross-sectional literature that is both significant and plausibly tradable; on Delta Exchange India's BTC/ETH/XAUT universe a cross-sectional strategy is not possible anyway (too few names), so for HapieCoin the practical relevance is as a BTC-vs-ETH relative signal at best.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study reports a net-of-cost Sharpe for crypto cross-sectional momentum; Han-Kang-Ryu is the closest and I only saw its abstract.
- LTW's published JF version may differ slightly from the NBER draft I read; figures above are from the NBER text.

## Key Question 5: What happened to crypto trend following in 2022-2026? Did it survive the 2022 drawdown and the 2024-2025 cycle?

### Takeaway
2022 was good for trend (the slow 60%+ decline was the textbook bear market trend followers profit from: Quantpedia's MAX rule stayed effective out of sample from Feb 2022, Pythagoras' trend token fund was reportedly +8% while the market collapsed); 2024 was good for long-biased trend (the bitcoin-focused Anna Fund topped the Nordic Hedge Index); 2025 was bad: realised vol fell to 43%, 30-day returns flipped sign repeatedly, the 10 Oct 2025 liquidation cascade ($19 bn) hit, and Bloomberg described 2025 as a year in which "violent swings erased months of positioning" for crypto trend funds, before a 13.3% rebound month in early 2026 as BTC fell 10%+ and ETH 18%.

### Cited Findings
- **[FIRM/BLOG]** Quantpedia's out-of-sample block, 4 Feb 2022 to 20 Aug 2024: the MAX (x-day high breakout) rule "stays effective" through the 2022 drawdown, mean-reversion (MIN) did not. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)
- **[FIRM, company-reported]** Pythagoras' trend-following token fund was reportedly up about 8% in 2022 while shorting crypto on trend indicators; the CEO said the firm "particularly outperforms in bear markets". — [inkl / original article](https://www.inkl.com/news/how-two-crypto-hedge-funds-dodged-the-market-collapse) (403 on fetch; from search summary)
- **[FIRM]** Coinbase Institutional, "Trend following in uncertain times", Sep 2022: found "significant variability in trend-following performance in recent quarters" and that top-line figures "obscure how sensitive crypto trend strategies are to timing and entry and exit levels". — [Coinbase Institutional](https://www.coinbase.com/institutional/research-insights/research/monthly-outlook/trend-following-in-uncertain-times-september-2022) (403 on fetch; from search summary)

- **[FIRM]** Bloomberg, 27 Feb 2026: choppy 2025 crypto markets punished trend-following funds, "with violent swings erasing months of positioning"; XBTO's trend fund then rose 13.3% in a month (its second-best since launching Feb 2024) as Bitcoin fell more than 10% and Ether 18%. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-27/crypto-trend-following-trade-finds-relief-after-sharp-selloff) (paywalled; from search summary)
- **[FIRM]** Crypto hedge-fund aggregates 2025 (Crypto Insights Group via InvestmentNews): directional funds -2.5% through November (worst since 2022, when many fell 30%+); fundamental/altcoin-heavy funds about -23%; market-neutral about +14.4%; Sigil Core (altcoin) -6.73% after -61% in 2022. — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/crypto-chaos-jolts-hedge-funds-in-worst-year-since-2022-crash/263643). ### Inferences
- The pattern matches the traditional-market evidence exactly: trend profits in slow, persistent moves (2022 down, 2024 up) and bleeds in range-bound or V-shaped years (2023 for 50/200-type rules, 2025). A rule that traded well 2022-2024 should be expected to have had a losing 2025 unless it is very slow or very fast.
- The 10 Oct 2025 cascade is a failure mode trend following does not hedge: a weekly-rebalanced trend system "should not be expected to hedge a crash lasting only a few days" (Dao et al., Q1).

### Gaps
- No audited crypto-trend index exists; all 2022-2026 performance is fund-level and self-reported (Pythagoras, Anna Fund, XBTO) or press-summarised (Bloomberg). Exact 2025 calendar-year returns for any named crypto trend fund were not found.

## Key Question 6: Which published rules are documented precisely enough to replicate, and with what results?

### Takeaway
Seven rule families are specified well enough to code: (a) long BTC when price > N-day SMA else cash, N in {5,10,20,50,100} (Detzel et al., gross Sharpe 2.2-2.6, 2010-2017) and N in {20,65,150,200} (Monash, BTC 20-day net Sharpe 1.25 at 0.5% cost, 2016-2023); (b) buy on an x-day high, x in {10..50} (Quantpedia, no costs, 2015-2024); (c) sign of trailing 1/3/12-month return, vol-scaled to 10% (AQR, traditional markets); (d) SMA/EMA/DEMA crossover with hourly windows about 140-1000 h (Rozario, unstable out of sample); (e) Donchian ensemble with vol sizing on the top-20 liquid coins (Zarattini, net Sharpe >1.5, parameters in the paywalled paper); (f) weekly quintile sort on 1-4 week returns, long-short (LTW, about 3%/week gross); (g) 50/200-day crossover (blog, -82% drawdown).

### Cited Findings
- **Rule A1 (Detzel et al. 2018/2021)**: daily close; position = long BTC if P_t > SMA_N(t), else T-bills; N = 5, 10, 20, 50, 100; 18 Jul 2010-12 Dec 2017; gross Sharpe 2.6 to 2.2, max DD -66.4% to -70.3%, breakeven one-way cost 1.1% to 4.5%. — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/currency-trading/timing-bitcoin-with-smas/)
- **Rule A2 (Le and Ruthbah 2024)**: hold asset when price is above the prior-day SMA, else cash; N = 20, 65, 150, 200; S&P BTC/ETH/LargeCap-ex-MegaCap indices, 2016/2017/2019 to Jan 2023; frictions 0.1/0.25/0.5%; BTC N = 20: gross Sharpe 1.68, net 1.25 at 0.5%. — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/applying-simple-trend-following-rules-to-cryptocurrencies)
- **Rule B (Quantpedia MAX)**: daily open; buy when today's price is the highest of the prior x days (x = 10-50), hold for the fixed forward window; Gemini data, 2015-2024; no costs; MAX held up Feb 2022-Aug 2024 with 20-day beating 10-day OOS. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)
- **Rule C (Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen)**: for each market, sign of trailing 1-, 3- and 12-month excess return, equal-weighted across the three lookbacks, each position scaled to equal ex-ante volatility (rolling 3-year covariance), portfolio targeted to 10% annual vol, monthly rebalance; 1880-2013 net Sharpe 0.77 across 67 markets. — [AQR PDF](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/03/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing.pdf)
- **Rule D (Rozario et al. 2020)**: hourly Bitstamp BTCUSD; long when short-window MA > long-window MA, else sell/flat; SMA, EMA (alpha = 2/(N+1)) and DEMA = 2*EMA - EMA(EMA); optimal windows 141h/781h (SMA), 721h/951h (EMA), 791h/981h (DEMA) over 2011-2019 with Sharpe 1.09/1.35/1.32 in-sample; walk-forward optima unstable, "window sizes show little trend with date with large variations in both long and short windows from 200-1000 hrs". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12155v1.pdf)
- **Rule E (Zarattini et al. 2025)**: ensemble of Donchian channels of several lookbacks (not disclosed in abstract), signals combined, volatility-based sizing, rotating top-20 liquid coins; net Sharpe >1.5, alpha 10.8%/yr vs BTC. — [abarbon.com](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends)
- **Rule F (Liu-Tsyvinski-Wu)**: each week, sort coins with cap > $1m into quintiles on past 1-, 2-, 3- or 4-week return; long Q5 / short Q1; rebalance weekly; 2.5-4.1% per week gross, 2014-2018. — [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882.pdf)
- **Rule G (Talyxion 2026)**: rate-of-change over lookback L on 6-hour bars; long when ROC > theta, short when < -theta (L and theta re-optimised monthly by grid search); ATR trailing stop S_t = max(S_{t-1}, P_t - 2.5*ATR_t); 70% long / 30% short equal weight; monthly selection requiring rolling Sharpe >= 1.3 (longs) / 1.7 (shorts). Several parameters (k for ATR, L grid, K_S) are not disclosed. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)
- **Rule H (blog 50/200)**: long when SMA50 > SMA200 else flat, 0.1% commission; -82.4% max drawdown. — [crypto-threads blog](https://crypto-threads-five.vercel.app/post/bitcoin-death-cross-a-data-driven-performance-study)

### Inferences
- Rules A1/A2/B are fully specified and cheap to replicate on Delta Exchange daily data; Rule C is the institutional benchmark to port (sign of 1/3/12-month return, 10% vol target).
- Rules D and G show that parameter re-optimisation on short windows does not transfer year to year in BTC; the fixed, simple rules are the ones whose out-of-sample record is documented.

### Gaps
- Zarattini et al.'s exact Donchian lookbacks and vol target are only in the paywalled SSRN text.

## Key Question 7: What do fees, slippage and funding do to the results?

### Takeaway
Costs matter in proportion to rebalancing frequency: a 20-day SMA rule on BTC lost about a quarter of its Sharpe (1.68 to 1.25) at 0.5% per trade; weekly cross-sectional momentum with 26-100 bp round-trip costs and liquidation risk loses most or all of its edge; and a long perpetual position in a bull market pays funding that has averaged about 16% annualised (Q1 2024) and spiked to 66% annualised (Jan 2024) and 100%+ (Feb 2024), which alone can exceed the trend premium.

### Cited Findings
- **[WP]** Monash: BTC 20-day SMA gross Sharpe 1.68 to net 1.25 at 0.5% friction (about -26%). — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/applying-simple-trend-following-rules-to-cryptocurrencies)
- **[PR]** Detzel et al.: breakeven one-way switching costs 1.1% (5-day SMA) to 4.5% (100-day SMA); CXO cautions that Bitcoin's historical frictions and execution delays may have exceeded the lower end. — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/currency-trading/timing-bitcoin-with-smas/). Detzel et al. (2020) are cited elsewhere for Bitcoin transaction costs of 0.1% to 0.3%. — [arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)
- **[PR]** Hudson and Urquhart: breakeven costs "well above the levels typically seen in cryptocurrency markets" in-sample, yet no out-of-sample predictability for Bitcoin. — [Reading repository](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/85715)
- **[PR, abstract]** Han, Kang, Ryu: with exchange fees, tick sizes, slippage and margin liquidation modelled, "many momentum portfolios end up liquidated" and many significant gross strategies become insignificant. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11156-024-01260-w.pdf)
- **[WP]** Talyxion sensitivity: Sharpe 2.87 at 0 bp, 2.41 at 4 bp, falling to 1.62 at 12 bp taker fee, with slippage linear in trade size over 5-minute volume and 8-hourly funding charged. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)
- **[PR]** Tzouvanas et al. assume 26 bp per trade for crypto momentum portfolios. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v191y2020ics0165176519303647.html)
- **[FIRM]** Funding: global average BTC perpetual funding hit a record 66% annualised on 1-2 Jan 2024 (Matrixport data; "longs pay shorts 66% per year to stay long", Markus Thielen). — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2024/01/02/bitcoin-bullish-bets-costlier-than-ever-as-funding-rates-hit-record-66/). Funding "jumped to 100%" annualised in Feb 2024. — [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/amp/cryptocurrencies/news/bitcoin-funding-rates-jump-to-100-sparking-opportunity-for-savvy-traders-202402270607). Q1 2024 average about 0.015% per 8h, roughly 16% annualised; "normal bull market" 0.005-0.02% per 8h (5-22% annualised); frothy phases 0.05-0.20% per 8h (55-200%+). — [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-perpetuals-funding-rate-analysis-the-0-01-benchmark-and-its-impact); [Kingfisher guide](https://thekingfisher.io/en/blogs/funding-rate-calculator) (vendor pages)
- **[FIRM]** Dated futures basis in the last bull market: "anywhere from 10-20% annual basis". — [Delphi Digital](https://members.delphidigital.io/feed/are-perpetuals-the-end-game-for-derivatives) (members page; from search summary)
- **[BLOG]** Worked example: 50% BTC spot gain over 6 months at 0.02% average funding nets about 44% on a perpetual long (about 6% funding drag). — [Kingfisher](https://thekingfisher.io/en/blogs/funding-rate-calculator)

### Inferences
- For a long-only BTC trend rule on perpetuals, a 10-20% annualised funding drag during exactly the periods the rule is long (bull markets) is the same order of magnitude as the excess return trend adds over buy-and-hold in most studies; spot or dated futures (basis 10-20% in bulls, per Delphi) avoid the spike risk but not the carry. Long-short books partly net funding (shorts receive it).
- Delta Exchange India fees are not in any of these studies; the project's own figure (0.01% option fee, 3.5% premium cap, GST, per the repo's GAPS #129) should be substituted when replicating.

### Gaps
- No study was found that reports a BTC or ETH trend-following result net of actual historical funding rates on a specific exchange other than the Talyxion preprint (Binance, 2022-2024).

## Key Question 8: Which parameter choices (lookbacks 1-12 months, 20/50/200-day MAs, volatility targeting) held up out of sample?

### Takeaway
Short daily lookbacks (10-65 days) are the ones with documented out-of-sample survival on BTC (Quantpedia MAX 10-50 day through 2022-2024; Monash 20-day best net Sharpe; Detzel 5-100 day all beat buy-and-hold gross), the classic 50/200 crossover shows an 82% drawdown, and intermediate 1-month / 3-month TSMOM on a crypto basket delivered Sharpe 0.5-0.65 with -35% to -38% drawdowns (preprint); volatility scaling raised that to 1.83 with -16% drawdown in the same test, consistent with AQR's 10%-vol design.

### Cited Findings
- **[WP]** Monash: shorter lookbacks (20-day) gave the highest Sharpe, and costs erode the shorter rules most. — [Monash](https://www.monash.edu/business/mcfs/our-research/all-projects/investment-strategy/trend-following-strategies-for-crypto-investors)
- **[FIRM/BLOG]** Quantpedia: any x from 10 to 50 days "is a reasonable choice"; OOS (Feb 2022-Aug 2024) 20-day beat 10-day. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)
- **[PR]** Detzel et al.: Sharpe monotonically declines from 5-day (2.6) to 100-day (2.2) but every length beat buy-and-hold on Sharpe and drawdown; the authors/CXO flag data-snooping across lengths. — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/currency-trading/timing-bitcoin-with-smas/)
- **[WP]** Rozario et al.: optimal hourly windows 200-1000 h with no stable pattern across years; "no predictable and attractive Sharpe ratios" from year-Y optimisation applied to year Y+1. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12155v1.pdf)
- **[WP]** Talyxion OOS 2022-2024 (net): TSMOM-1M Sharpe 0.65 / MDD -34.8%; TSMOM-3M 0.54 / -38.2%; vol-scaled TSMOM (10% target) 1.83 / -16.1%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)
- **[FIRM]** AQR's century design uses equal weight on 1-, 3- and 12-month lookbacks with 10% portfolio vol targeting; a CBS thesis supervised by Pedersen notes the 2012 MOP paper found a 3-month lookback produced the best results in futures. — [AQR PDF](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/03/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing.pdf); [CBS thesis](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/60699508/815971_Masters_thesis_final_Magnus_Bojesen.pdf)
- **[FIRM]** Harvey et al., "The Impact of Volatility Targeting" (60+ assets, no crypto): vol scaling improves Sharpe for equities, negligible effect for bonds, currencies, commodities, but reduces extreme returns everywhere. — [Quantpedia blog listing](https://quantpedia.com/blog/page/100/)
- **[BLOG]** 50/200 crossover BTC: -82.4% max drawdown, 1,662 days under water, only 10 signals in six years. — [crypto-threads blog](https://crypto-threads-five.vercel.app/post/bitcoin-death-cross-a-data-driven-performance-study)
- **[PR]** Cross-sectional horizon: 1-4 week sorts significant, 8-100 week not (LTW); reversal from 4-6 weeks (Dobrynskaya). — [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882.pdf); [HSE](https://spb.hse.ru/en/news/559533243.html)

### Inferences
- For BTC the evidence favours fast rules (10-65 day breakouts or SMAs) over the equity-style 12-month lookback or 200-day MA; the 200-day rule's edge is drawdown avoidance in 2022-type declines, not Sharpe.
- Vol targeting is the single largest reported improvement (Sharpe 0.5-0.65 to 1.83 in the one direct test), and it is the mechanism AQR uses; it should be treated as part of the rule, not an add-on.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed test isolates volatility targeting on a BTC trend rule; the only direct comparison is the Talyxion preprint.
- No ETH-specific parameter study found.

## Key Question 9: What are the known failure modes (whipsaw, post-publication decay, crowding, funding)?

### Takeaway
Documented failure modes are: range-bound/V-shaped markets (2023 for slow rules, 2025 for all; realised vol 43% with sign-flipping 30-day returns), fast crashes shorter than the rebalance horizon (10 Oct 2025, $19 bn liquidated), parameter instability when optimised on one year (Rozario), post-publication decay (58% average for equity anomalies; Hudson-Urquhart found Bitcoin's in-sample predictability vanished out of sample), funding/basis drag on bull-market longs (16-66%+ annualised in 2024), and altcoin liquidity evaporation that wiped out altcoin quant books in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **[FIRM]** Whipsaw 2025: "Strategies relying on volatility expansion or sustained trends faced frequent whipsaw"; BTC 30-day rolling returns "alternated repeatedly between positive and negative". — [XBTO](https://www.xbto.com/resources/2025-the-year-market-regime-mattered-more-than-market-direction)
- **[BLOG]** Slow-rule whipsaw: the 50/200 crossover "struggled to remain long during fast upside expansions in 2021 and again in 2023". — [crypto-threads blog](https://crypto-threads-five.vercel.app/post/bitcoin-death-cross-a-data-driven-performance-study)
- **[FIRM]** Crash-speed limit: "A trend system rebalanced weekly should not be expected to hedge a crash lasting only a few days." — [Quantpedia summary of Dao et al.](https://quantpedia.com/tail-protection-of-trend-following-strategies/)
- **[FIRM]** 10 Oct 2025: about $19 bn liquidated in 24 hours, BTC $122k to $102k intraday; altcoin quant models "total wipeouts". — [XBTO](https://www.xbto.com/resources/2025-the-year-market-regime-mattered-more-than-market-direction); [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/crypto-chaos-jolts-hedge-funds-in-worst-year-since-2022-crash/263643)
- **[WP]** Parameter instability: Rozario et al. (Q6/Q8). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12155v1.pdf)
- **[PR]** Decay: McLean-Pontiff 26% OOS / 58% post-publication; Hudson-Urquhart "no predictability for Bitcoin in the out-of-sample period". — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/effects-of-market-adaptation); [Reading repository](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/85715)
- **[FIRM]** Crowding/capacity: Dao et al. see "little evidence of overcrowding, though capacity is an open question" (traditional CTAs); AQR attribute weaker 2000-2013 results to fewer independent trends and rising correlations, not crowding. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/tail-protection-of-trend-following-strategies/); [AQR PDF](https://oxfordstrat.com/coasdfASD32/uploads/2016/03/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing.pdf)
- **[FIRM]** Funding: 66% annualised record Jan 2024; "very high funding rates become a burden for longs when the market stops moving higher" and can force unwinds. — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2024/01/02/bitcoin-bullish-bets-costlier-than-ever-as-funding-rates-hit-record-66/)
- **[FIRM]** Sensitivity to entry/exit: Coinbase Institutional (Sep 2022) on "how sensitive crypto trend strategies are to timing and entry and exit levels". — [Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/institutional/research-insights/research/monthly-outlook/trend-following-in-uncertain-times-september-2022)
- **[FIRM]** Stablecoins: "One should be very cautious about applying traditional momentum models to pegged currencies" (Andre Rzym, Man AHL). — [CryptoGlobe](https://cryptoglobe.com/latest/2024/04/forget-hodling-quantitative-hedge-funds-use-crypto-volatility-to-further-gains)

### Inferences
- The failure modes are the same as in futures trend following plus two crypto-specific ones: funding carry on the long side and liquidation cascades that complete inside one daily bar.
- Any HapieCoin backtest should report performance separately for 2023 and 2025 (range years) and include the 10 Oct 2025 bar; a rule that only shows 2022 and 2024 is cherry-picked.

### Gaps
- No quantitative crowding measure for crypto trend (AUM in systematic crypto trend, or signal correlation across funds) was found; Crypto Insights Group sizes all active liquid crypto strategies at $12-15 bn.

## Key Question 10: Can trend be expressed with options (call/put spreads) instead of futures, and is there evidence it is better?

### Takeaway
The literature establishes that trend following is mechanically a long-straddle/strangle replication (95.8% monthly return correlation between a 12-month S&P trend rule and a delta-replicated straddle; a trend model has "exactly the same exposure to the long-term variance as a portfolio of naked strangles"), but every source concludes the futures/delta version is cheaper because trend pays realised-variance "whipsaw cost" while options pay implied-variance premium, and most of trend's historical return comes from the trading-impact term that an option buyer does not capture. No study was found that tests trend-following with BTC or ETH options, and I did not locate an Israelov-Nielsen paper on this topic.

### Cited Findings
- **[FIRM]** Hoffstein (Newfound Research), "Trend: Convexity and Premium", Feb 2019: a monthly S&P 500 model (long on positive 12-month return, short on negative) and a monthly-rebalanced delta-straddle model have a **95.8% correlation in monthly log returns**; the binary signal and the straddle delta converge about 0.6 standard deviations from the strike with monthly rebalancing, about 1.2 with quarterly; using the Bruder-Gaussel / Jusselin et al. decomposition, "most of the historical premium comes from trading impact, not convexity"; a straddle buyer "pays an up-front premium tied to implied volatility" while trend "pays trading costs tied to realized volatility and the price path"; the option strategy lagged pure trend slightly. — [Newfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/02/trend-convexity-premium/); [7 Circles summary](https://the7circles.uk/?p=37769). In a separate Newfound test, trend following outperformed explicit option purchases for most lookbacks over a 15-year period except the March 2020 expiration. — [Newfound option tag](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/option)
- **[FIRM]** Dao et al. (CFM 2016): "a simple trend model has exactly the same exposure to the long-term variance as a portfolio of naked strangles"; the strangle's payoff is model-independent and matches a variance swap; "Options offer a better hedge in principle, but their premium is too high"; long-vol portfolios lost money for two decades while trend was positive; some convexity is lost at the diversified-CTA level. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/tail-protection-of-trend-following-strategies/); [CFM](https://www.cfm.com/tail-protection-for-long-investors-convexity-at-work/)
- **[FIRM]** CFM "The Convexity of Trend Following": trend is mechanically convex relative to the underlying but CTA implementation steps dilute it; convexity is "a bonus feature rather than the core source of return". — [CFM](https://cfm.com/the-convexity-of-trend-following/); [Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/the-convexity-of-trend-following/)
- **[WP, non-crypto]** A 2024 Padova thesis on Italian electricity derivatives found trend following dominates for small-to-moderate moves while the straddle wins in extreme moves. — [Padova thesis](https://thesis.unipd.it/handle/20.500.12608/102307)
- **[FIRM]** Quantpedia "Trend Model via Difference Between Long- and Short-Term Variance" (Bouchaud, Dao et al.): trend P&L is proportional to long-term minus short-term variance, the same exposure as being long a long-dated and short a short-dated variance swap. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/?p=680)

### Inferences
- For HapieCoin (options-only on Delta India), the replication logic says: a trend signal expressed as a long call spread (uptrend) or long put spread (downtrend) buys defined convexity and caps whipsaw loss at the premium, but gives up the trading-impact return and pays implied vol, which on BTC/ETH typically exceeds realised vol. The literature's expectation is therefore lower average return than a futures trend rule, with better behaviour in gap events like 10 Oct 2025 that futures trend cannot hedge.
- A hybrid consistent with the evidence: use the trend signal to pick direction and use options only when implied vol is low relative to realised vol (the cost condition both Newfound and CFM identify).

### Gaps
- I found no Israelov & Nielsen paper on trend following with options; their known work (Israelov-Nielsen "Still Not Cheap", "Covered Calls Uncovered") concerns index-option selling, not trend replication. This may be a misattribution in the brief.
- No empirical study of trend following expressed through BTC or ETH options (Deribit or Delta) was found; no backtest of call/put spreads driven by a trend signal in crypto exists in the sources reached.
