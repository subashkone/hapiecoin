## Key question 1: Cash-and-carry and funding arbitrage: what annualised yields did the BTC basis trade produce year by year 2020-2026, and is the Schmeling-Schrimpf-Todorov finding (carry is large, predictable and crash-prone) correct?

### Takeaway
No source gives a clean calendar-year table of realised basis-trade returns; what exists is a set of dated snapshots that agree on the shape: 3-month basis of 40-46% annualised on offshore venues and about 12% on CME at the April 2021 top, a collapse to 3-8% by June 2021, near-zero and negative CME basis from August 2022 through the FTX collapse, 15-25% on CME at the February and November 2024 peaks, below 5% by February 2025, briefly negative in March and November 2025, and about 5% against a 4.5% risk-free rate by April 2026. Over 2021-2025 the mean implied quarterly carry on Binance was roughly 8.7% (BTC) and 8.2% (ETH), and perpetual funding averaged 7.8-9.2% annualised over 2021-2024 with 16-18% of days negative. The BIS/Management Science paper's core claims (carry averaging above 10% a year, peaks of 40-60%, driven by trend-chasing leveraged small investors and scarce arbitrage capital, and high carry predicting crashes) are supported by every independent data point found; what no public source reports is the net-of-fees, net-of-liquidation realised Sharpe of actually running the trade.

### Cited Findings

**The Schmeling, Schrimpf and Todorov paper and what it claims**
- The paper "Crypto carry" is BIS Working Paper No. 1087 (first published 4 April 2023, 54 pages, revised October 2025) by Maik Schmeling, Andreas Schrimpf and Karamfil Todorov; it defines carry as the futures-minus-spot gap, i.e. the return on a long-spot short-futures cash-and-carry trade in Bitcoin and Ethereum. **[BIS/REG]** — [BIS WP 1087 landing page](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)
- The 2023 abstract says crypto carry "can become very large (up to 60% p.a.)" and is strongly time-varying; the Contribution section says it averages "above 10% annually" and is much larger than carry in equities, fixed income, currencies and commodities. **[BIS/REG]** — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm); [Mondo Visione summary](https://mondovisione.com/news/bis-crypto-carry-202344/)
- The October 2025 CEPR Discussion Paper 20719 version of the same paper softens the peak to carry that "sometimes exceed[s] 40% per annum", attributes it to "demand from smaller, trend-chasing investors seeking leveraged exposure" and to arbitrage capital constrained by regulatory and margin frictions, and interprets it as a large, volatile "inconvenience yield" of holding spot. **[BIS/REG]** — [IDEAS/RePEc CEPR DP 20719](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/20719.html)
- A version titled "Crypto Carry" is listed at Management Science (DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069); the page returned HTTP 403 so the published abstract and numbers were not seen. **[PR-abs, not visible]** — [INFORMS listing](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/references/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069)
- The paper's findings as stated on the BIS page: interest-rate differentials "explain very little" of the variation; the convenience yield driving carry comes from (a) trend-chasing and attention by smaller investors seeking leveraged upside in booms and (b) scarcity of arbitrage capital on the cash-and-carry side; cash-and-carry is risky "because margins spike and liquidations occur during drawdowns"; crypto futures show "excess volatility" relative to spot; "high crypto carry predicts future price crashes" and rising carry coincides with a rising price of crash-risk insurance. **[BIS/REG]** — [BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)
- Cointelegraph's report on the paper adds: only about 3% of carry size is explained by interest-rate differences between crypto and fiat or between exchanges; rising CME carry was associated with rising net long positions of "nonreportable" traders (family offices, prop firms, wealthy individuals) during strong trends and heightened media attention; sell-side arbitrage capital is "scarce and slow-moving" because it bears price-volatility risk. **[NEWS]** — [Cointelegraph on the BIS study](https://cointelegraph.com/news/smaller-investors-can-have-outsized-impact-on-crypto-investment-markets-bis-study)
- Difference-in-differences results from the paper as relayed by a Bocconi student club article: after CME micro bitcoin futures launched on 3 May 2021 (1/10 BTC vs 5 BTC standard), the relative carry gap between CME and other exchanges rose about 11%; after the January 2024 spot ETF launch, average crypto carry fell about 3 percentage points across exchanges and about 5 additional points on CME. **[BLOG relaying PR]** — [BSIC, 16 Nov 2025](https://bsic.it/?p=19265)
- The paper's full text (sample period, named venues, regression coefficients, crash hit-rates, any strategy Sharpe) could not be retrieved: the BIS PDF URL serves an HTML bot-challenge page to automated fetchers, and both the CEPR and INFORMS full texts are gated. Statements above are abstract-level only. **[gap]**

**Independent multi-year carry measurements (2021-2025)**
- Using Binance USD-margined quarterly futures vs spot daily closes, January 2021 to October 2025, with annualised continuously compounded carry ln(F/S)/(T-t) and a 7-day rolling median: mean implied carry BTC 8.68%, ETH 8.18%; S&P 500 futures implied carry 3.47% over the same window (2.12% dividend-adjusted); crypto carry standard deviation about 8.5-10% (roughly four times the S&P's); skewness BTC about 2.4, ETH about 2.2 vs -0.46 for the S&P; carry "especially high in 2021 and from the second half of 2023 through 2024"; a regression of crypto carry on the Fed funds rate gives R-squared about 0.013. No fees or funding included; it is implied carry, not realised P&L. **[BLOG with stated method]** — [BSIC, 16 Nov 2025](https://bsic.it/?p=19265)
- Same source, liquidation simulation of the short-futures leg at a 2.5% maintenance margin over 2021-2025: liquidated 16% of the time at 5x leverage, 47% at 10x, 79% at 20x, 89% at 25x. **[BLOG]** — [BSIC](https://bsic.it/?p=19265)

### Inferences
- The year-by-year picture, stitched from the snapshots above and stated as a range rather than a measured average: 2020 (no direct data found other than Franz-Valentin's pre-2018 15% CIP deviations collapsing); 2021 offshore quarterly basis 40%+ in Q1-April, single digits after May, low single digits by Q3-Q4 (K33 July 2021 lows of 1.3%); 2022 near zero to negative for most of the year (CME front-month -3.36% in August, record discount in November); 2023 recovery from backwardation to positive but no level figures found; 2024 CME 15-25% at the February and November peaks with perp funding spiking to 66% in January; 2025 compression to below 5% by February, briefly negative in March and November, around 10% in May; 2026 to April about 5% against a 4.5% risk-free rate. Treat this as the shape of the series, not a table of annual averages.
- The Schmeling et al. qualitative claims are corroborated by independent data: the 2021-2025 Binance mean implied carry of 8-9% and the Ethena funding mean of 7.8-9.2% both sit near or just below the paper's "above 10%" average (the paper's 2019-2022 sample likely includes the 2021 extreme); the collapse of carry after the ETF launch and the CF Benchmarks correlation of 0.5 between basis and momentum both fit the "trend-chasing leveraged demand" mechanism; and every high-carry episode in the record (April 2021 at 46%, January 2024 at 66% funding, November 2024 at 20%+) preceded a drawdown within weeks to months, which is the "carry predicts crashes" pattern (the paper's own statistics could not be read, so the strength of the predictive relationship is unverified here).
- For a cash-and-carry trader the gross carry (8-10% average, 15-25% at peaks) has to cover: taker fees on two legs, funding reversals (16-18% of days negative, streaks of 13 days documented, one 66-day streak in 2025), the margin-spike liquidation risk on the short leg (16% liquidation incidence even at 5x in the BSIC simulation), and the opportunity cost of the risk-free rate (4.5-5% in 2025-26). After the ETF launch the net spread over Treasuries was repeatedly zero or negative in 2025-26, which is why CME open interest fell to a 14-month low in April 2026.

- No source provides calendar-year realised returns (net of fees and funding) for a BTC cash-and-carry strategy 2020-2026; Glassnode, Kaiko, Coinbase Institutional, Galaxy Research, Laevitas, Amberdata and CoinGlass pages with such a table were not found in search (a Galaxy search returned only hedge-fund launch news and 13-F aggregators).
- The full text of Schmeling-Schrimpf-Todorov (sample period, exchanges, crash-prediction coefficients, Sharpe of the strategy) was not readable; the BIS PDF endpoint blocks automated fetching and the Management Science page returned 403.
- 2020 and 2023 carry levels: no dated figures were found except qualitative "recovered from backwardation" statements.
- Franz and Valentin: only the Quantpedia summary of the abstract was visible.

## Key question 2: Funding-rate-based directional strategies: does contrarian (against extreme funding) or momentum (with funding) have documented results?

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study of directional funding signals was found; the evidence is K33's six-event "negative 30-day funding precedes +79% average 90-day return" statistic and two 2026 blog backtests on Binance data that disagree: a 2022-2026 z-score contrarian on BTC earned +6.3bp per 8-hour trade net of 4bp fees (Sharpe 1.32) but decayed to negative expectancy in 2025 and lost money on ETH, while a December 2025 to February 2026 bucket test found extreme positive funding behaved as momentum (price up 71% of the time over 24 hours) and extreme negative funding was a coin flip. The honest reading is that naive funding contrarianism has decayed, extreme positive funding is not reliably a short signal at 24-hour horizons, and only stress-conditioned variants show positive expectancy, on small samples.

### Cited Findings
- K33: the 30-day average perp funding turning negative (seventh time since 2018, September 2024) was followed historically by average 90-day BTC returns of 79% and a median of 55%; the article presents it as a case for a bottom, not a guarantee, on a six-event sample. **[FIRM via NEWS]** — [The Block on K33](https://www.theblock.co/post/315831/perp-signal-bitcoin-bottom-bullish-year-end-k33)
- Blog backtest (Binance BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT perps, 1 Jan 2022 to 25 Feb 2026, 8-hour funding intervals, signal = rolling 90-interval z-score of funding, short if z > 1, long if z < -1, hold one interval, 4bp round-trip cost, no slippage): BTC baseline 1,438 trades, +6.31bp per trade, Sharpe 1.32, terminal equity 2.07x; ETH baseline 1,390 trades, -2.54bp, Sharpe -0.39, 0.51x; BTC by year +19.65bp (2022), +6.68 (2023), +1.59 (2024), -0.65 (2025), +8.24 (2026 YTD); a filtered variant (long only when z < -1 and one-week realised vol is in the top quartile) made 230 trades at +21.25bp, Sharpe 3.06, 1.53x, and +20.88bp in 2025. Author's words: "Execution cost is the whole game." **[BLOG]** — [askrubyai, 26 Feb 2026](https://askrubyai.github.io/blog/posts/2026-02-26-funding-regime-filter/index.html)
- Blog test (Binance BTCUSDT, 10 Dec 2025 to 14 Feb 2026, 197 funding events, 24-hour forward return, no fees modelled): after deeply negative funding (below -0.005%, n=8) average 24h return -0.479% and price up 50% of the time; after very positive funding (at or above 0.01%, n=14) average +0.383% and up 71.4% of the time; ETH after extreme negative funding -0.847% (n=20), SOL -1.533% (n=56, up only 38%), DOGE -0.530% (n=30). Author's conclusion: extreme negative funding is not a reliable contrarian buy, funding mean-reverts while price often does not, and funding is more useful for carry than for direction. **[BLOG, tiny sample]** — [askrubyai, 14 Feb 2026](https://askrubyai.github.io/blog/posts/2026-02-14-funding-rate-contrarian)
- The CF Benchmarks finding that the CME basis correlates 0.50-0.54 with momentum z-scores and that moderating short-term momentum often precedes basis narrowing "within several trading days" (no statistics given) is the institutional analogue: basis/funding is a lagging function of trend, not a leading contrarian indicator at short horizons. **[FIRM]** — [CF Benchmarks](https://www.cfbenchmarks.com/blog/revisiting-the-bitcoin-basis-how-momentum-sentiment-impact-the-structural-drivers-of-basis-activity)
- A TradingView funding-rate strategy script uses BTCUSDT 1-hour bars 2022-2023 with 0.10% commission per side and 0.05% slippage; its source is protected and no results were visible. **[BLOG, results not seen]** — [TradingView script](https://www.tradingview.com/script/u4emyPXd/)
- Trading-education sites assert that sustained negative funding (-0.03% or worse) is a contrarian buy "at capitulation extremes" without published backtests. **[BLOG, unsupported]** — [thekingfisher.io](https://thekingfisher.io/en/blogs/funding-rate-guide)

### Inferences
- The two blog backtests are consistent with each other once horizons are separated: contrarian fading of crowded positioning worked in 2022-2023 (bear-market squeezes), decayed in 2024-2025, and short-horizon (24h) positive funding is momentum rather than reversal. The K33 statistic operates at a 30-day smoothing and 90-day horizon, so it is a cycle-bottom indicator rather than a tradable signal; six events cannot establish expectancy.
- At Delta Exchange India taker fees (0.05% per side plus 18% GST, about 11.8bp round trip) the naive BTC contrarian expectancy of +6.3bp per trade (computed at 4bp cost) would be negative; only the stress-filtered variant (+21bp gross of its 4bp) would survive, and that rests on 230 trades.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or research-firm backtest of funding-rate directional signals with fees was found; Glassnode, Kaiko, Velo and CoinGlass publish funding charts but no strategy returns appeared in search.
- The 26.7% vs 54.5% win-rate figures quoted in the search excerpt for the February 2026 blog do not appear in that article's tables and could not be verified.

## Key question 3: Mean reversion and pairs/stat-arb in crypto: academic and practitioner evidence; does short-horizon BTC mean reversion exist and survive fees?

### Takeaway
Short-horizon mean reversion in BTC and altcoins is real and robustly detectable (90% of 183 Binance pairs show significant 15-minute directional reversal in every coin-year since 2021, versus 2.7% of US stocks) but the gross edge is about 1.3bp per trade against a 5bp round-trip spot cost, so it is not exploitable as a standalone taker strategy; pairs trading on 26 Binance coins underperformed benchmarks and was very sensitive to costs and parameters, with 5-minute versions (11.6% a month gross in one specification) doing far better than daily (-0.07% a month). Older papers claiming profitable hourly Bollinger or autocorrelation strategies generally did not model costs carefully.

### Cited Findings
- Kitron and Wengrowicz (arXiv 2608.21888, submitted 22 Aug 2026): at 15-minute horizons 90% of 183 Binance pairs show significant directional reversal versus 2.7% of 187 US stocks and ETFs, in every focal coin-year since 2021; lag-one autocorrelation is near zero for major coins but betting against the previous candle's sign captures most of the effect; reversal concentrates after aggressive-taker-flow moves and grows with flow intensity; US-listed funds whose NAV tracks a crypto or metal price inherit the reversal; the contrast survives a permutation null and a frozen six-month holdout (class-mean AUC gap +0.011, 95% CI +0.008 to +0.014 under conservative accounting); gross edge peaks near 1.3bp per trade against an assumed 5bp round-trip cost, so "too small to clear benchmark spot capture costs"; thresholding to higher-confidence signals shrinks the opportunity faster than it raises per-trade edge. **[PR-abs, preprint]** — [arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21888)
- Fil and Kristoufek, "Pairs Trading in Cryptocurrency Markets", IEEE Access 8 (2020) 172644-172651, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3024619: distance and cointegration methods on 26 liquid Binance coins at 5-minute, 1-hour and daily frequencies; "the strategies underperform classical benchmarks", results "quite sensitive to parameter settings and external factors such as transaction costs or execution windows"; the most common daily distance method returned -0.07% monthly while the 5-minute version returned 11.61% monthly; intraday prices show simple mean reversion absent in daily data. Whether the 11.61% is gross or net of fees was not visible. **[PR-abs]** — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/8fbfa5ffeaae44a5a4c635f23f5ca05c); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9200323)
- Tadi and Kortchemski (arXiv 2109.10662, 2021): dynamic cointegration pairs trading on BitMEX using Engle-Granger, KSS and Johansen tests with an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck half-life to set the look-back; minute-binned signals, backtest on best bid/ask and market trades with execution conditional on available size; they report beating buy-and-hold on BitMEX, better risk-adjusted returns from multi-coin spreads, and "reasonably low" max drawdown; no return, Sharpe, drawdown or fee figures are in the abstract. **[PR-abs, preprint]** — [arXiv 2109.10662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10662)
- A 2025 volatility-forecasting paper that ran a pairs backtest found Johansen cointegration rank 2 for its crypto group but only ADA-ETH stationary pairwise (p = 0.045), so the backtest used one pair. **[PR-abs, preprint]** — [arXiv 2508.01880](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01880)
- Amberdata blog (Part 4 of a series) backtests an ETC/FIL spread in USDT growing from 1,000,000 to about 1,620,000 over several years, with no transaction-cost analysis visible. **[FIRM blog]** — [Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/empirical-results-performance-analysis)

### Inferences
- The combined evidence says the reversal is a liquidity-provision premium: it is captured by makers who are paid the spread, not by takers who pay it. For a retail trader on a taker fee of 5bp plus GST per side, a 1.3bp gross edge is a guaranteed loss; the only route is passive limit orders (Delta's 0.02% maker fee) and even then the edge is a few basis points per trade.
- Pairs/cointegration results improving sharply from daily to 5-minute frequency (Fil-Kristoufek) is the same pattern: the profit is in microstructure noise, which in turn is where fees bite hardest. Daily-frequency stat-arb among top altcoins has no documented positive expectancy.

### Gaps

- No study was found that tests Bollinger-band or RSI mean reversion on BTC with realistic perpetual fees and funding over 2022-2026 other than the student thesis.
- Tadi-Kortchemski performance numbers not visible.

## Key question 4: Cross-exchange arbitrage and the Kimchi premium: Makarov-Schoar and later work; what is left after 2021?

### Takeaway
Makarov and Schoar (JFE 2020) documented large, recurrent cross-exchange price gaps that were much bigger across countries than within them (pointing to capital controls), widened when BTC rose, and co-moved; follow-up work (Crepelliere, Pelster and Zeisberger, Journal of Financial Markets 2023; Shynkevich 2023) finds the profits "greatly decreased" and arbitrage opportunities "reduce substantially after 2018". The Kimchi premium, 54% at its January 2018 peak and about 20% in Makarov-Schoar's sample, still spikes in bull episodes (10% in September 2024, highest since May 2021) but ran 1-3% in calm 2024 months, about 0.16% in February 2026 and occasionally negative; it is not capturable by foreigners because of Korean capital controls, so what is left is a sentiment indicator, not a trade.

### Cited Findings
- Makarov and Schoar, "Trading and arbitrage in cryptocurrency markets", Journal of Financial Economics 135(2), 2020, 293-319, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.07.001: cryptocurrency markets show "large, recurrent arbitrage opportunities" across exchanges; price deviations are much larger across countries than within them and smaller between cryptocurrencies, consistent with capital controls limiting arbitrage capital; cross-country deviations co-move and widen when bitcoin appreciates, more so in countries with higher premia; the common component of signed volume explains 80% of bitcoin returns and the idiosyncratic components help explain spreads between exchanges. **[PR-abs]** — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:2:p:293-319)
- Secondary coverage of the same paper: opportunities "often persist for weeks" and the Korean price was documented as much as 20% above the US price. **[NEWS]** — [Decrypt, Professor Coin](https://decrypt.co/245963/professor-coin-free-lunch-cryptocurrency-markets)
- Crepelliere, Pelster and Zeisberger, "Arbitrage in the Market for Cryptocurrencies", Journal of Financial Markets, DOI 10.1016/j.finmar.2023.100817: confirms the earlier findings but shows "the magnitude of arbitrage profits has greatly decreased"; a later 16-exchange study cites it and Shynkevich (2023) as showing "arbitrage opportunity reduces substantially after 2018". **[PR-abs via secondary]** — [Paderborn record](https://ris.uni-paderborn.de/record/34449); [Cardiff ORCA paper citing both](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/173664/1/1-s2.0-S154461232401393X-main.pdf)
- Franz and Valentin: CIP deviations up to 15% until Q1 2018, then a decline attributed to the entry of sophisticated arbitrageurs, with remaining deviations tracking exchange credit risk. **[PR-abs via Quantpedia]** — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/crypto-covered-interest-parity-deviations/)
- Kimchi premium peak: 54.48% in January 2018. **[NEWS/glossary]** — [BabyPips](https://www.babypips.com/forexpedia/kimchi-premium)
- Kimchi premium 2024: stayed within 1-3% in early 2024, then rose to about 8.5% against Binance ($72,974) and briefly 10% in September 2024, "a peak not seen since May 2021"; observers regard 5% as already high. **[NEWS]** — [Korea Times, Sep 2024](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/www/nation/2024/09/602_370612.html)
- Kimchi premium 2026: about 0.16% over Binance in February 2026 when BTC dipped below 90 million won on Upbit; other 2025-26 readings cited at 1.06%, -1.54% and -1.52% (dates not confirmed). **[NEWS, low confidence]** — [MEXC news relay](https://www.mexc.com/news/648090); [Bloomingbit](https://en.bloomingbit.io/feed/news/88244)

- Blockworks' "What happened to arbitrage trading in crypto?" returned HTTP 403 and was not read. **[gap]** — [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/what-happened-to-arbitrage-trading-in-crypto)

### Inferences
- Within-currency, within-jurisdiction cross-exchange arbitrage (Binance vs OKX vs Bybit USDT prices) was already compressed to market-maker spreads by 2018-2019 per Franz-Valentin and Crepelliere et al.; what persisted into 2021 was cross-currency/cross-border premia protected by capital controls (Korea) and exchange credit risk (FTX, 2022). Since 2021 the only recurring cross-exchange edge is the premium on exchanges with onboarding or credit risk, which is compensation for that risk, not free money.
- For an INR-funded trader the Indian analogue is a potential INR premium/discount on Delta India versus offshore USD venues, which (like the Kimchi premium) is bounded by capital-flow frictions (LRS limits, 1% TDS on VDA transfers, 30% flat tax) and cannot be arbitraged freely; no source measuring an "Indian premium" on BTC perps was found.

- No quantitative post-2021 series of cross-exchange USD price dispersion from Kaiko or similar was found.
- No source on an India-specific BTC premium on Delta Exchange India vs global venues.

## Key question 5: Seasonality in BTC/ETH: day-of-week, hour-of-day, weekend, US session, month-end and options-expiry effects; which are documented with statistics and did they persist?

### Takeaway
Volatility and volume seasonality is well documented and strengthening: volatility peaks around 16:00 UTC and bottoms near 05:00 UTC, spikes at 00:00 and 16:00 (less at 08:00) that the authors link to perpetual funding timestamps, a large burst in the first minute of every hour (strongest every fourth hour), and Saturday volatility 0.3-0.5 log-points below average. Return seasonality is contested: Caporale-Plastun (Finance Research Letters 2019) found a Bitcoin Monday effect for 2013-2017, Quantpedia's Gemini 2015-2022 sample found 22:00-23:00 UTC the strongest hours (33% annualised, Sharpe 1.58, costs not included) and Friday the strongest day, NYDIG found Friday and early-week outperformance, but Baur, Cahill, Godfrey and Liu (Finance Research Letters 2019, 15 million observations, seven exchanges) found "no consistent or persistent patterns" and conclude the market is weak-form efficient. No study tests whether the hour-of-day return effects survived 2022-2026, and no systematic study of returns around the Friday 08:00 UTC Deribit expiry was found.

### Cited Findings

**Volatility and volume seasonality (strongest evidence)**
- Hansen, Kim and Kimbrough, "Periodicity in Cryptocurrency Volatility and Liquidity" (arXiv 2109.12142, Sep-Nov 2021): Binance and Coinbase Pro (BTC, ETH) plus Uniswap V2 (ETH only), detailed sample 1 Oct 2020 to 1 Jun 2021 with centralized-exchange data back to 1 Jan 2019; average annualised realised volatility about 88% BTC and 111% ETH; volatility "tends to be high around 16 UTC and hit bottom near 5 UTC"; spikes in volume and volatility at 00h and 16h with "less noticeable spikes at 8h" on all exchanges and both coins; hours after 16:00 UTC and the first hour of the day have the highest volatility, the first hour particularly on Mondays; Coinbase has substantially less volume 05-12 UTC; weekend volatility and volume are lower, most pronounced on Saturday (GARCH Saturday coefficients roughly -0.3 to -0.5 log scale); within the hour, a large volatility burst in the first minute, medium bursts after minutes 15, 30 and 45, smaller bursts every five minutes, the effect "most pronounced every fourth hour, 00h, ..., 20h, with the exception of 08h", which the authors link (as conjecture) to perpetual funding at 00/08/16 UTC on Binance, hourly on FTX and 04/12/20 on BitMEX; "these patterns have grown stronger over the years"; the paper does not test signed-return seasonality or US equity open effects. (Note: a search summary attributed this paper to "Aleti and Mizrach"; the arXiv page lists Hansen, Kim and Kimbrough.) **[PR-abs plus full text via ar5iv, preprint]** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.12142); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.12142)

### Inferences
- The hierarchy of evidence is clear: volatility/volume seasonality (funding timestamps, top-of-hour bursts, 16:00 UTC peak, Saturday trough) is documented in a large-sample paper and corroborated by Kaiko's volume data, and it strengthened rather than decayed after publication; return seasonality (Monday, Friday, 22:00-23:00 UTC) rests on single-exchange or pre-2018 samples, conflicts across papers, lacks cost accounting, and has no published post-2022 out-of-sample test. The only safe use of seasonality for a trader is volatility timing (when to expect gamma, when spreads widen, when to avoid resting orders), not directional bets.
- Since 2024 the structural shift to US-hours and weekday concentration (46% of volume in US hours, 13% at weekends) means the 16:00 UTC volatility peak is now also the ETF-flow window and Monday 00:00 UTC carries the weekend gap, which fits Hansen et al.'s "first hour particularly high on Mondays". The Kaiko warning about thin-weekend cascades is the relevant seasonality for a leveraged perpetual trader.
- For a Delta India trader (IST = UTC+5:30): 16:00 UTC is 21:30 IST, 00:00 UTC is 05:30 IST, 08:00 UTC Deribit settlement is 13:30 IST, US equity open 13:30/14:30 UTC is 19:00/20:00 IST. ## Key question 6: Traditional-market analogues: commodity carry/roll yield, FX carry and Treasury basis trades, and what their crash episodes teach

### Takeaway
Carry is a documented premium in every traditional asset class: Koijen-Moskowitz-Pedersen-Vrugt (JFE 2018) report long-short carry Sharpe ratios of about 0.7-0.8 per asset class and about 1.2 diversified (working-paper drafts), Daniel-Hodrick-Lu (CFR 2017) put the G10 FX carry Sharpe at 0.78 over 1976-2013 with large, long drawdowns and negative skew, Gorton-Hayashi-Rouwenhorst find backwardated commodities beat an equal-weight index by 5.4% a year (1969-2006), and the Treasury cash-futures basis trade grew to roughly $1 trillion of short futures notional by 2024 while unwinding violently in March 2020 when hedge funds net sold about $173B of Treasuries. The common lesson is that carry is compensation for liquidity, volatility and funding risk that arrives all at once: the March 2020 basis unwind, the 2008 FX carry crash and the May 2021 / November 2022 crypto basis collapses are the same mechanism with different leverage.

### Cited Findings
- Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt, "Carry", Journal of Financial Economics 127(2), 2018, 197-225: carry (an ex-ante, model-free characteristic) predicts returns cross-sectionally and over time in global equities, global bonds, currencies, commodities, US Treasuries, credit and options; it is not explained by known predictors; carry strategies share exposure to "global recession, liquidity, and volatility risks" but none fully explains the premium; a generalised UIP and the Expectations Hypothesis are rejected. **[PR-abs]** — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:127:y:2018:i:2:p:197-225)
- Working-paper drafts of the same paper: a 2016 draft reports an average annualised Sharpe of about 0.8 for the long-short carry strategy in each asset class and about 1.2 for a diversified portfolio across asset classes; a 2013 draft gives about 0.7 per asset class; a third-party guide puts cross-asset carry correlations at roughly 0.1-0.3 (unconfirmed). Published-version figures not seen. **[PR drafts via secondary]** — [2016 draft on Scribd](https://s.veneneo.workers.dev/https/www.scribd.com/document/787520127/Pedersen-2016-Carry); [2013 draft on SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/WilhelmFritsche/carry-37306328); [NBER w19325](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/19325.html)
- Daniel, Hodrick and Lu, "The Carry Trade: Risks and Drawdowns" (NBER WP 20433, 2014; Critical Finance Review 6(2), 2017, 211-262): baseline G10 carry trade annualised Sharpe 0.78 over February 1976 to August 2013, alternative versions up to 1.02; "carry trade drawdowns are large and occur over substantial time intervals"; dollar-neutral carry is highly negatively skewed with considerable downside risk while a diversified dollar-carry portfolio shows minimal skewness and no downside risk; standard equity, bond, FX, volatility and downside-equity factors do not explain the profits; negative skew at longer horizons is driven by time-varying autocorrelation. **[PR]** — [Columbia PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/6378/Daniel.Hodrick.Lu.Carry%20Trade.Critical%20Finance%20Review.2017.pdf); [NBER w20433](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20433)
- Brunnermeier, Nagel and Pedersen, "Carry Trades and Currency Crashes" (NBER w14473): returns to standard FX carry trades are negatively skewed (cited by Daniel-Hodrick-Lu). **[PR via citation]** — [NBER w14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473.pdf)
- Gorton and Rouwenhorst (Financial Analysts Journal 62(2), 2006): fully collateralised commodity futures historically offered about the same return and Sharpe as US equities. Gorton, Hayashi and Rouwenhorst, "The Fundamentals of Commodity Futures Returns": a portfolio of commodities in backwardation outperformed an equal-weighted index by 5.4% a year and contango commodities underperformed by 4.8% a year (1969-2006 sample); the return is attributed to bearing volatility risk when inventories are low; collateral return is the largest component of total futures return, then roll yield. Erb and Harvey dispute the existence of a commodity risk premium, attributing it to rebalancing. **[PR via secondary summaries]** — [EconPapers FAJ 2006](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:ufajxx:v:62:y:2006:i:2:p:47-68); [CXO summary of GHR](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/classic-paper-physical-inventories-and-commodity-futures-returns); [Morningstar on Erb-Harvey](https://www.morningstar.com/funds/rethinking-commodities)
- Ghoddusi (energy futures): a conditional roll strategy (long in backwardation, short in contango) delivers the highest Sharpe for all commodities tested. **[PR via Quantpedia]** — [Quantpedia roll-yield maturity structure](https://quantpedia.com/effect-of-maturity-structure-of-roll-yields-in-commodity-futures-strategies/)
- Federal Reserve FEDS Note (8 March 2024), "Quantifying Treasury Cash-Futures Basis Trades": leveraged funds' short Treasury futures notional reached $913B (Sep 2023) and $991B (Jan 2024), "well over $1 trillion" by end-2023; TRACE-proxy basis positions at least $317B since Q1 2022, peaking at $352B in Nov 2023, with net-repo upper bound $574B; hedge funds bought a net $428B of Treasuries during 2017-2019 QT, 91% by likely basis traders; in March 2020 hedge funds net sold about $173B of Treasuries, concentrated in likely basis traders; the basis spiked in March 2020 then stayed near zero or negative until mid-2022 and was "again profitable" in 2H 2022; the trade is "typically highly leveraged" with margin, rollover and risk-limit risks that cause rapid large unwinds. No return or leverage ratio figures. **[BIS/REG]** — [Federal Reserve FEDS Note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/quantifying-treasury-cash-futures-basis-trades-20240308.html)
- OFR Brief 2020-01 on basis trades: large losses at relative-value hedge funds engaged in basis trades followed from illiquidity in March 2020 and those funds significantly unwound; Fed researchers link the rapid unwinding to Treasury market stress that month. PDF was binary and unreadable; statements from the search excerpt. **[BIS/REG, excerpt only]** — [OFR brief](https://www.financialresearch.gov/briefs/files/OFRBr_2020_01_Basis-Trades.pdf)
- 2025: hedge funds reportedly pulled back from the Treasury basis trade "as spreads dry up". **[NEWS]** — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/hedge-funds-treasury-basis-trade-pullback/)
- A low-authority education page quotes FX carry Sharpe about 0.5-0.8, a 2008 max drawdown near 30% (another table on the same page says about 55%) and a worst month of -8.2% in October 2008; internally inconsistent, unverified. **[BLOG, unreliable]** — [FFF Institute](https://fffinstill.com/learning/concepts/the-carry-trade)

### Inferences
- The traditional carry Sharpe of 0.7-0.8 per asset class is earned on carry of a few percent a year with low volatility; crypto carry of 8-10% average comes with 8.5-10% standard deviation of the carry itself and a short leg on an asset with 88-111% realised volatility, so the headline yield is higher but the ratio of carry to margin-call risk is not obviously better. The Treasury basis trade earns tens of basis points at 50-100x leverage; crypto basis earns 5-25% at 1-5x. Both die the same way: a volatility spike forces margin calls on the futures leg while the hedge is illiquid.
- The Koijen et al. finding that carry loads on "global recession, liquidity and volatility risks" and Schmeling et al.'s "high carry predicts crashes" are the same statement: carry is paid for being short volatility and liquidity. Daniel-Hodrick-Lu's point that diversification (dollar-carry basket) removes the negative skew has no crypto analogue because BTC and ETH carry are nearly perfectly correlated.

### Gaps
- Published JFE 2018 Sharpe table not seen; figures are from 2013/2016 drafts.
- No dollar loss figures for March 2020 basis-trade unwinds or for 2008 FX carry drawdowns were found in a primary source.
- Gorton-Hayashi-Rouwenhorst and Erb-Harvey full texts not accessed; figures from secondary summaries.

## Key question 7: Relevance for a trader on Delta Exchange India (perpetuals plus daily/weekly/monthly BTC/ETH options, INR-funded, taker 0.05% futures, 0.01% options capped at 3.5% of premium, 18% GST); which strategies survive the fees and which do not

### Takeaway
Delta India's own pages confirm futures 0.02% maker / 0.05% taker, options 0.01% of notional for maker and taker capped at 3.5% of premium (one page shows "3.5% 10%" side by side, so the current cap should be re-verified), 18% GST on all trading fees (0.05% becomes about 0.059%), settlement at taker rates and a Scalper Offer that waives the closing fee on BTC/ETH futures closed within 30 minutes. At roughly 11.8bp round-trip taker (or 4.7bp maker), the strategies that survive on paper are: funding/basis carry only when funding exceeds about 8-10% annualised (so that fees, GST, 16-18% negative days and INR opportunity cost are covered), with no spot leg available on Delta so the hedge must be an options synthetic or an offshore spot position; volatility-timing of option sales using the documented 16:00 UTC peak and 05:00 UTC trough; and stress-conditioned funding squeezes if traded passively. Strategies that do not survive: 15-minute mean reversion (1.3bp gross edge), naive funding contrarianism (+6bp at 4bp cost, negative in 2025), daily-frequency altcoin pairs trading (not offered and negative anyway), cross-exchange INR arbitrage (capital-control bound), and hour-of-day return seasonality (uncosted, untested since 2022).

### Inferences
- Fee arithmetic (own calculation from the first-party rates): a perpetual round trip at taker is 2 x 0.05% x 1.18 = 0.118% of notional; at maker 2 x 0.02% x 1.18 = 0.047%; with the Scalper Offer a sub-30-minute BTC/ETH futures round trip at taker costs 0.059%. An options trade on a premium worth 1% of notional pays min(0.01%, 3.5% x 1%) = 0.01% of notional, i.e. 1% of premium (1.18% with GST); for a premium below 0.286% of notional the cap binds and the fee is 3.5% of premium (4.13% with GST), which is the regime of far-OTM daily options.
- Funding carry on Delta: there is no spot BTC on Delta India for the long leg (spot is listed at 0%/0.1% but the product is INR-settled derivatives), so a Delta-only carry trade must be short perp vs long synthetic (long call, short put at the same strike) or a calendar vs a dated future. The synthetic costs two option fees (about 0.02-0.08% of notional with GST depending on the cap) plus its own basis, and the perp leg 0.047-0.118% round trip; against an average gross funding of 8-9% a year that is affordable (under 0.3% a year of fees if held for months), but the trade still eats the 16-18% negative-funding days, the 13-66 day negative streaks, the margin-spike liquidation risk on the perp leg, INR funding cost, 30% flat tax on gains with no loss offset and 1% TDS on transfers. It is viable only in the 15-25% funding regimes (early 2021, January and November 2024) and zero-to-negative at 2025-26 levels of about 5% against a 4.5% dollar risk-free rate and a higher INR rate.
- Strategies killed by fees at Delta taker rates: 15-minute reversal (1.3bp gross vs 11.8bp cost), naive funding contrarian (+6.3bp gross vs 11.8bp), 5-minute pairs trading (not offered; the Fil-Kristoufek edge is a microstructure edge the taker never sees). Strategies killed by structure rather than fees: Kimchi/INR premium arbitrage (capital controls), CME basis (no CME access from INR). Strategies plausibly surviving: (1) harvest funding only above a threshold near 10% annualised with the perp leg placed at maker and hedged with an options synthetic, exiting on funding flips rather than riding them; (2) sell options into the documented 16:00 UTC (21:30 IST) volatility peak and buy back in the 05:00 UTC (10:30 IST) trough only if the repo backtest confirms it after the 3.5%-cap fee, given the memory note that most daily structures lose; (3) stress-conditioned long entries after extreme negative funding in high-vol regimes, sized for the 10 October 2025 type of cascade, treated as a candidate rather than a proven edge.
- The relevant failure modes for a Delta user are the same four as offshore: exchange/counterparty risk (FTX showed the hedge venue itself can fail; Delta India's segregation and FIU status mitigate but do not remove this), funding flips (sign reversal for weeks), liquidation cascades in thin weekend/Asian hours when US participants are absent (Kaiko's 13% weekend volume), and basis blowouts at expiry when settlement is charged at taker rates on every open contract.
